Axios reported the letter said any talks to prevent new strikes must include Iran's nuclear program. The President said on October 28 the outreach concerned medical supplies, not renewed talks. (S-004)

[E-LOW] Reuters reported that Qatari mediators handed Iran a new draft deal on October 25; a senior Iranian official denied a new proposal and called Qatar's ideas "unacceptable." Tasnim said Tehran would drop none of its core conditions, including lifting the blockade, easing sanctions, and access to frozen assets. A Qatari delegation returned to Tehran on October 30. (S-008, S-009, S-040)

[E-MODERATE] Foreign Minister Araghchi said on October 31 that a proposal discussed with mediators could reopen the Strait of Hormuz within two days, and that Tehran has no immediate plans to meet U.S. officials directly. The United States rejected Iran's earlier seven-day reopening plan. Al Jazeera's summary quotes Araghchi calling the Vance letter a "positive step." (S-006, S-005)

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: A signed or jointly announced framework by November 30 is very unlikely (EF-057-P10), and a publicly confirmed direct meeting by November 15 is unlikely (EF-057-P5). The base rate is one signed instrument in eight months of war. Neither channel touches the core trade: Iran wants the blockade lifted first, and Washington wants enrichment on the table. The two-day reopening offer is a price signal aimed at the post-midterm decision, not a change in sequencing. A public Iranian reply to either text by November 4 is a roughly even chance (EF-057-P2). The observable that would change this: a named venue and date for talks.
