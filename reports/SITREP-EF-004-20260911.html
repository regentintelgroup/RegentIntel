SECTION 1: BLUF

The center of gravity in the Iran conflict shifted in the last 48 hours from the Strait of Hormuz to the Bab el-Mandeb. Houthi forces completed a rapid push to take control of Yemen's Red Sea coastline on September 11, capturing Perim Island inside the Bab el-Mandeb Strait a day after seizing the port of Mokha, following an advance on Dhubab and the apparent withdrawal of Yemeni government forces. Simultaneously, Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Energy confirmed that its cross-country oil pipeline was struck by projectiles and shut down as a precautionary measure, removing the principal workaround Riyadh had been using to bypass a Hormuz corridor that remains effectively closed. This is the most consequential single-day degradation of global energy logistics since the war began on February 28, 2026, because it attacks the redundancy rather than the primary route. On the diplomatic track, the IAEA Board of Governors voted 23 to 3 with eight abstentions on September 9 to refer Iran to the UN Security Council over nuclear non-compliance, with Russia, China, and Niger opposed. Regent Intel Group assesses that the Bab el-Mandeb development creates a near-term decision point for Washington on opening a Yemen line of operation, and that the highest-probability escalation vector over the next 72 hours is a US or Saudi strike campaign against Houthi coastal assets rather than further exchanges in the Gulf [E-HIGH / P-MODERATE].

SECTION 3: STRATEGIC OVERVIEW

3.1 Iran Nuclear Program and IAEA Reporting

[GOV] The IAEA Board of Governors on September 9 referred Iran to the UN Security Council for the first time in twenty years, with 23 votes in favor, China, Russia, and Niger against, and eight abstentions; the resolution was co-sponsored by the United States, United Kingdom, France, and Germany and found Iran in non-compliance with its safeguards obligations. The text requests the Director General to report Iran to the Security Council and the General Assembly, notes concern at what it calls "Iran's continued non-compliance with its obligations," and calls on Tehran to engage in talks without preconditions. [E-HIGH]

[RESEARCH] At the Security Council, Beijing and Moscow are not expected to support new penalties such as sanctions, but the IAEA action carries a political message independent of US preferences. Assessment: the referral is procedurally significant and operationally inert in the near term. Its practical value to Washington is as a legitimizing predicate for continued unilateral military and economic action, not as a pathway to multilateral enforcement [E-HIGH / P-MODERATE]. Underlying assumption: Russian and Chinese veto behavior remains consistent with their September 9 Board vote. If either abstains at the Security Council rather than vetoing, the assessment of the referral as symbolic requires revision.

[GOV] Grossi told the opening of the Board meeting in Vienna on September 7 that the Agency is unable to verify the status of Iran's declared nuclear material and facilities due to lack of access and information, describing it as a "matter of serious proliferation concern." [PRESS] Since February 2026, the IAEA has been unable to visit any Iranian nuclear facility other than the Bushehr nuclear power plant, and inspectors have not visited the sites bombed in June 2025 either. [E-HIGH]

[RESEARCH] Iran's stock of more than 9,000 kilograms of enriched uranium remains inaccessible at Natanz, Fordow, and Isfahan, the regime is not believed to have recovered it, and whether the stocks survived the strikes is not known. [PRESS] Iran was estimated to hold roughly 440.9 kilograms of uranium enriched to 60 percent before the latest military escalation; that level is not weapons-grade but can be further enriched. [E-MODERATE] Evidentiary confidence is moderate rather than high because these are pre-strike estimates carried forward without verification, and no inspection has occurred since.

[PRESS] On September 10 the President threatened the Pickaxe Mountain site for the fifth time on the same day CSIS published satellite analysis finding more road activity at the site in 2026 than at any point in its history. Assessment: the convergence of repeated presidential targeting rhetoric with independent imagery analysis of site activity makes Pickaxe Mountain the most likely next fixed-target strike objective in the nuclear portfolio [E-MODERATE / P-MODERATE]. Explicit assumption: that road activity at an underground construction site indicates programmatic activity rather than repair, dispersal, or deception. That assumption is not verifiable from OSINT and is the single weakest link in this line of reasoning.

3.2 IRGC and Proxy Activity

Houthis (primary development)

[WIRE] The Houthis seized the Red Sea city of Mocha on September 10 as internationally recognized government forces withdrew, with three Yemeni government sources confirming the capture to Reuters. [WIRE] On September 11, Houthi forces captured Mayun, also known as Perim, a small volcanic island inside the strait, confirmed by a senior Yemeni military official; the capture represents the Houthis' biggest territorial gains in years along the Bab el-Mandeb. [PRESS] Mokha lies about 50 miles from the Bab el-Mandeb Strait. [E-HIGH]

[WIRE] In a September 11 statement the Houthi armed forces claimed coastal gains without mentioning the island, Mokha, or the strait by name, vowed "escalation for escalation" against Saudi Arabia, and declared that navigation is safe for all companies except Saudi vessels. [E-HIGH]

Behavioral analysis note. The Houthi military communique omitted the two most operationally significant gains of the week. Applying communications-pattern tradecraft to publicly observable messaging, deliberate omission of a captured objective from a claim-maximizing actor is more consistent with an intent to avoid triggering an immediate US or Saudi response against that objective than with an intent to publicize leverage. Regent Intel Group assesses the omission is deliberate signaling restraint on the chokepoint specifically, paired with maximal rhetorical escalation against Saudi Arabia [E-MODERATE / P-MODERATE]. Alternative explanation not excluded: the claim was drafted before the Perim landing was confirmed to Houthi leadership.

Iraqi Shia militias

[PRESS] Regarding the Saudi pipeline attack, it was not immediately clear who was responsible or whether the pipeline itself was damaged, but one US official said it was struck by drones originating from Iraq. [RESEARCH] Iran-allied militia groups in Iraq have previously used drones against Saudi Arabia and may have done so again as part of a broader Iranian strategy of using proxies to push Arab states to reject American forces, according to FDD's Behnam Ben Taleblu. [E-MODERATE] Evidentiary confidence is moderate: the attribution rests on a single anonymous US official quoted in one outlet, and the Saudi government has not attributed the attack.

[GOV] The US Treasury announced new sanctions on September 10 against firms and individuals in Iraq, the United Arab Emirates, Lebanon, and Turkey that it says support Kata'ib Hezbollah, an Iraqi paramilitary group under IRGC command, and Hezbollah in Lebanon. [E-HIGH] Assessment: the geographic spread of the designations, particularly the inclusion of UAE and Turkish nodes, indicates US targeting is now focused on the financial intermediation layer outside Iraq and Lebanon rather than on in-country facilitators [E-HIGH / P-MODERATE].

[PRESS] Kurdish authorities said they intercepted ten explosive-laden drones in the Erbil area during the September 2 exchange. [E-MODERATE]

Hezbollah and Lebanon

[RESEARCH] A full Israeli ground invasion occurred in March 2026, leaving Israel in control of roughly 20 percent of Lebanese territory with over one million Lebanese displaced; a June US-brokered framework conditioned Israeli withdrawal on Hezbollah disarmament, which the group rejected, leaving Israeli forces entrenched in southern Lebanon. [RESEARCH] The IDF said it struck Hezbollah positions in southern Lebanon in response to two explosive drones aimed at Israeli troops near the Ali al-Taher ridge, the third such attack in a week. [E-HIGH]

[PRESS] In the preceding reporting window the Lebanese front was the war's deadliest ground and the Israeli operation there ran without Iranian reference; an Israeli drone struck a motorcycle at Zebdine in the Nabatieh district on September 9 killing the rider, with Lebanon's National News Agency reporting the death and the Israeli military issuing no statement, while pre-dawn strikes hit several southern towns and Israeli forces demolished a school at Mansouri. [WIRE] AFP imagery from September 11 documents the aftermath of an Israeli demolition on the Ali al-Taher ridge that triggered an earthquake, and an Israeli drone carrying explosives for detonation on houses in Mansouri village. [E-HIGH]

Source symmetry note. [GOV -- ALLIED] The Israeli military has issued no statement on the September 9 Zebdine strike while confirming other operations in the same district. Regent Intel Group documents this asymmetry in official acknowledgment without inferring intent from it. Allied silence on a specific engagement is an observable, not an admission, and is recorded here under the same standard applied to Iranian non-acknowledgment of proxy operations.

Assessment: the Lebanese front is being prosecuted on a separate logic from the Iran campaign. Israeli operations there are demolition-and-attrition focused and are not being sequenced against events in the Gulf [E-HIGH / P-HIGH]. This means Lebanon is unlikely to serve as the trigger for horizontal escalation in the next 14 days, and it also means a Gulf ceasefire would not automatically terminate the Lebanese front.

3.3 US Force Posture in the CENTCOM AOR

[TRADE] Less than a month after taking up station in the Middle East, elements of the George Washington Carrier Strike Group reportedly came under IRGC ballistic missile fire on September 5 according to Iranian media, and CENTCOM confirmed that "A U.S. aircraft carrier and guided-missile destroyer successfully evaded multiple unprovoked Iranian attacks," without identifying the warships. [TRADE] [ADVERSARY-SOURCED] Iranian-affiliated channels claimed the IRGC fired the Qassem Basir medium-range anti-ship ballistic missile for the first time; if true this would be the first known combat employment of the system since its May 2025 unveiling, with a stated range of 1,200 kilometers that is more than likely overstated. [E-MODERATE]

[TRADE] US cruisers and destroyers can engage anti-ship ballistic missiles in two layers using SM-3 in midcourse and SM-6 in terminal phase; both are finite and magazine-limited. Assessment: sustained Iranian ASBM employment against carrier strike group escorts imposes a magazine-depletion problem that is the principal constraint on indefinite US naval presence inside the Gulf, more binding in the near term than platform attrition [E-MODERATE / P-MODERATE]. Explicit assumption: that at-sea replenishment of VLS cells is not routinely available in the current threat environment. This is a standing limitation of US surface combatant design and is not a classified judgment.

[GOV] CENTCOM stated that on September 1 the US military completed a wave of strikes against the IRGC targeting air defense sites, radar systems, maritime assets, mine-laying capabilities, and communication sites. [PRESS] The US military destroyed five Iranian crude oil tankers on September 8 in retaliation for attempted attacks on an American warship, which CENTCOM said evaded the attack with no American personnel harmed. [E-HIGH]

Allied claim versus observable record. [GOV -- ALLIED] The President described many Iranian tankers as destroyed and referenced a figure of nine ships; that figure does not reconcile with the ten hulls counted by one open-source tracker since September 1, and CENTCOM has published no cumulative tanker figure of its own. Regent Intel Group records this as an unresolved discrepancy between a senior allied official statement and the observable open-source record. It is documented here under the same standard applied to Iranian claims, and no inference of intent is drawn. The absence of a published CENTCOM cumulative figure is itself an information gap (Section 8).

3.4 Strait of Hormuz Maritime Security

[TRADE] The Hormuz transit picture is actively contested: PortWatch's most recent published day, September 6, 2026, recorded 6 transits against a pre-crisis baseline of 85 per day. [PRESS] Al Jazeera's September 7 coverage was headlined on Hormuz traffic falling while the US insisted the waterway was "fully open." [E-MODERATE]

Source symmetry note. [GOV -- ALLIED] The US characterization of the strait as fully open is not reconcilable with a transit count of 6 against an 85 per day baseline. Regent Intel Group assesses the observable transit data over the official characterization [E-MODERATE / P-HIGH]. The discrepancy is most plausibly explained by a definitional difference (legal openness versus commercial usability) rather than by factual error, but that explanation is inference, not established fact.
