Section 1: BLUF

The Director of National Intelligence has told the Secretary of State in an unclassified memo that Iran has "substantially increased efforts to target U.S. personnel" and that lethal plots have "reached advanced stages" in the Middle East and "certain other countries." Section 3: Strategic Overview

3.1 Directed Attack Inside the United States

Definitions. "Directed": DOJ or FBI publicly attributes the attack to IRGC or proxy direction within 60 days of the event. "Strike resumption": any U.S. strike on Iranian territory as defined in SITREP-EF-056, which that product assesses as a roughly even chance before November 3; a limited strike counts.

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: We assess it is highly improbable (5 to 20 percent) that a directed lethal attack occurs inside the United States in the next 30 days if no U.S. strike on Iranian territory occurs. We have MODERATE confidence; a completed lethal attack in the United States would not be missed by collection, and the base rate is zero directed lethal attacks in 240 days of conflict, including the February to April period of active strikes. In the 30 days following any U.S. strike on Iranian territory, we assess it is improbable (20 to 45 percent), lower half. We have LOW confidence in the conditional judgment. Because the strike condition is itself roughly even, the unconditional 30-day chance sits in the highly improbable to improbable range. Basis: the DNI memo places advanced plots abroad, not at home; the directed network documented in the al-Saadi case relied on remote recruitment of individuals with no prior ties and was interdicted before any U.S. attack; UK authorities report more than 50 Iran-linked arrests in a year, indicating the network is most active in Europe. The single completed attack on a U.S. synagogue during the war (Temple Israel, West Bloomfield, March 12) was characterized by the FBI as "a Hezbollah-inspired act of terrorism," not directed. ([PRESS] Fox News Oct 24; [GOV] DOJ May 15, 2026; [PRESS] JTA Mar 31, 2026; [PRESS] The National Oct 19)

Alternative View: The network has already tasked U.S.-based individuals and is holding them for a strike trigger, which would move the conditional branch to roughly even or higher. The DNI's "certain other countries" language and the single-sourced allegation (The Times via The National) that al-Saadi continues to direct plots from detention support this; the latter is a flag, not a basis. It would become the lead assessment if DOJ unseals a complaint naming U.S.-based recruits, or if the FBI issues a public bulletin on IRGC recruitment via Telegram or Snapchat.

3.2 Attacks on U.S. Personnel Abroad

Definition: an attack on U.S. government, military, or diplomatic personnel or facilities abroad that the U.S. government attributes to Iran or an Iranian proxy within the window. U.S.-linked commercial shipping is excluded; it is covered in SITREP-EF-056 3.3.

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: We assess it is improbable (20 to 45 percent), upper half, that such an attack occurs in the next 30 days if no U.S. strike on Iranian territory occurs, and probable (55 to 80 percent), upper half, in the 30 days after any U.S. strike. We have LOW confidence in the first branch and MODERATE in the second. The no-strike branch rests on one allied-government origin, the DNI memo, and no attack on U.S. government targets has been retrieved since the April ceasefire. The memo was screened for friendly-side narrative shaping: it was released unclassified in the same week as the President's strike remarks and immediately preceded staffing actions, so it serves the record for both an ordered departure and a strike decision; it is also consistent with genuine warning. Both readings are carried. The strike branch rests on the IRGC's stated doctrine ("if a country attacks us, we will both respond and launch an offensive") and the ordered departure. ([PRESS] Fox Oct 24; [PRESS] ABC live Oct 23; [PRESS] Al Jazeera Oct 25; [WIRE] Reuters via Algemeiner Oct 23)

Alternative View: The plots described in the memo have already been disrupted and the memo documents a disruption rather than a pending attack, as such memos often do: roughly even on the no-strike branch. The memo's own language ("did not identify specific targets or timing") supports this. It would become the lead assessment if State lifts the Saudi ordered departure within 14 days or if DOJ announces charges tied to the plots.

We have MODERATE confidence. Basis: during the March to May 2026 period such events occurred at a rate of roughly one per month (Temple Israel, March 12, FBI: "Hezbollah-inspired"; al-Saadi directed plot against New York, Los Angeles, and Scottsdale institutions, charged May 15); the Israeli election and a possible strike provide trigger events; the Ashab al-Yamin propaganda apparatus remains in circulation. It would become the lead assessment if no qualifying document or statement is issued by November 15.

Section 5: Analytical Development

The Directed-Attack Model, Documented in Court

The al-Saadi complaint (SDNY, unsealed May 15, 2026) is the clearest open-source description of how Iranian-backed networks reach the U.S. homeland without a resident cell. Al-Saadi, described by DOJ as a senior Kata'ib Hizballah commander operating abroad, is alleged to have: posted calls to "jihad" on March 9, the day the first of at least 18 attacks in Europe began; directed the Bank of New York Mellon bombing in Amsterdam (March 15 to 16); sent an undercover officer photos and maps of a New York synagogue and two other Jewish institutions in Los Angeles and Scottsdale on April 3 and directed attacks on them; and coordinated arson and stabbing attacks in Skopje and London. The network brand, Harakat Ashab al-Yamin al-Islamiya, is described as a Kata'ib Hizballah component whose videos circulate on IRGC and Hezbollah-aligned Telegram channels. ([GOV] DOJ May 15, 2026)

What the model means for protective planning: targeting is remote, recruits are individuals without prior network ties, tasking includes reconnaissance imagery and maps of specific institutions, and the propaganda cycle is tight (video within one to four days of an attack). The Times, reporting on DOJ filings, says al-Saadi has continued to direct plots from detention through coded calls, about $1 million in transfers, and propaganda oversight. That allegation is unproven and disputed by his defenders; if accurate it means detention of a single commander did not stop the tasking function. ([PRESS] The National Oct 19, citing The Times)

Observable Indicators of Network Activation

Clients and law enforcement liaison should watch for: new Ashab al-Yamin or Kata'ib Hizballah video releases referencing U.S. cities; DOJ unsealing of complaints involving U.S.-based recruits; FBI or JTTF outreach to Jewish community institutions in New York, Los Angeles, or Arizona; reports of hostile reconnaissance (photography, mapping, repeated visits) at religious or Israeli-linked sites; and any FBI public statement about recruitment on Telegram or Snapchat. The United Kingdom reports more than 50 Iran-linked arrests in the past year and a RUSI-briefed assessment that Iran is "operating at the maximum of its capability in the UK"; European activity has preceded U.S. activity in this network by weeks.

Why the DNI Memo Matters Even Though It Is About Abroad

The memo is unclassified and was sent to State, which has acted on it with an ordered departure and staffing changes. Its silence on the homeland is a limit of the memo's purpose, not a finding of safety. The pattern in March 2026 was that an inspired attack on a U.S. synagogue followed the start of hostilities within two weeks without any federal advisory. We therefore key Homeland posture to the strike calendar in SITREP-EF-056 (a roughly even chance of a strike before November 3 and a roughly even chance of a campaign by December 31), not to federal advisories. Dates to carry: October 27 Israeli vote, November 3 midterms, end of November force arrival.

Section 6: Protective Guidance

All client categories

  • Treat October 28 as the posture-change date. The theater product assesses a roughly even chance of a U.S. strike before November 3 and names the Israeli result on October 28 as a branch point. Move to the pre-strike posture below by October 28 and hold through December 31 unless a signed agreement is announced.
  • Confirm JTTF or FBI field office liaison contact and the reporting path for suspicious activity before November 3.
  • Do not key posture to NTAS. Assume no federal advisory will precede an event.

Category A: Corporate security, U.S. facilities

  • Pre-strike posture: increase exterior patrol and camera review at perimeter and parking; brief lobby and mail staff on hostile reconnaissance indicators (repeated photography, questions about schedules, unattended vehicles); verify visitor management is enforced.
  • If a U.S. strike occurs: for 14 days, stand up a daily security call, restrict non-essential visitor access, and coordinate with local law enforcement on patrol frequency. Indicator for stand-down: 14 days without a federal charging document or FBI bulletin referencing Iran.
  • Jewish community institutions, Israeli-linked businesses, and Iranian-dissident organizations should apply the post-strike posture now, given 3.3.

Category B: Maritime, energy, logistics

  • Hormuz: assume ten or more attacks per week through November 8; the Oman-coast southern route carries 57 of 79 projectile incidents since July 6 and is not a safe alternative. Plan on Kpler flows near 68 percent of pre-war and on insurance repricing.
  • Saudi sites: the ordered departure applies to U.S. government personnel; apply the same standard to non-essential contractor staff in Riyadh, Eastern Province, and the southwest. Aramco has evacuated Dhahran HQ. Patriot coverage failed at Riyadh on October 10; do not assume interception.
  • Connectivity: for Gulf-hosted systems, confirm failover to regions outside the UAE and Bahrain and test it before October 28; the stated Iranian aim is to prevent repair.

Category C: Travel and duty of care

  • Saudi Arabia: defer non-essential travel; Etihad Saudi routes are cancelled through November 5 and flydubai through October 29. Riyadh airport is operating two terminals.
  • Israel: expect election-related disruption October 27 to 29 and a West Bank lockdown; no change to the Iran-strike risk window for travelers (see SITREP-EF-056 Section 7).
  • Iraq and Lebanon: no travel. UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait: travel permitted with a 24-hour departure plan; the trigger to depart is extension of the U.S. ordered departure to that country.

Category D: Financial and strategic risk

  • Plan Q4 and Q1 on Brent above $100 and on a roughly even chance of a U.S. campaign by December 31 with siege plus limited strikes as the main alternative; a signed agreement by November 30 is highly improbable.
  • Monitor two decision points: the Israeli coalition outcome (mid-November) and the Roosevelt group arrival (end of November).
