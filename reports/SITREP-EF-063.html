7.1 Iran Hits Bahrain With Missiles and Drones; a Civilian Plant Burns

[E-MODERATE] Bahrain's military said its air defenses intercepted a number of Iranian aerial attacks late on November 21, and the Interior Ministry reported incoming missiles and drones. It said a civilian industrial facility caught fire, with material damage and no injuries, and did not name the site. Sirens sounded at 2222 local time; the fire was reported at 2348. (S-001, S-002, S-003)

[E-LOW] Iranian accounts name different targets: the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) cited the al-Riffa and Awali bases, and Fars cited Sheikh Isa and Juffair. IRNA said an interceptor fired from the Fifth Fleet base crashed on Sitra Island and caused the fire. Neither Bahrain nor the U.S. military confirmed a hit on any base. (S-004, S-005, S-006, S-007)

Attribution rung reached: 6 (state direction) for the November 21 attack, which the IRGC claims as its own operation and Bahrain attributes to Iran.

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: For the first time in this round an Iranian attack left damage in a host state, though Iranian media blame interceptor debris for the fire. The damage fell on a civilian plant, not a base, so the threshold for a larger U.S. reply was not crossed. An Iranian launch at Bahrain or Kuwait reported by the host or CENTCOM by November 25 is likely (EF-063-P1). A U.S. casualty would change this.

7.2 Iran Readies a Kharg Tanker Convoy to Test the Blockade

[E-LOW] Iranian officials say more than 14 tankers are positioned off Kharg Island with crews assigned, ready to sail toward the Gulf of Oman. Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi called the blockade an act of war and said Tehran has warned Washington that the convoy will sail soon for China. (S-016, S-017, S-018)

[E-MODERATE] CENTCOM said the blockade reached 130 consecutive days on November 21. On November 20 the Treasury sanctioned four Iranians it called oil trustees for collecting revenue from illicit oil sales. Iran has warned of a fierce military response, including attacks on Gulf energy infrastructure, if U.S. forces engage the convoy. (S-020, S-029, S-019)

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: The convoy is Tehran's way of forcing Washington to choose between letting the blockade lapse and firing on laden tankers. U.S. forces disabled a tanker that ignored instructions near Kharg in July, and the President has repeated that runners will be sunk, so sailing carries a known price. Five or more laden tankers reaching the Gulf of Oman together by December 6 is unlikely (EF-063-P3). Two-origin tracking of laden tankers leaving Kharg together would change this.

7.3 Tanker Strikes Run Near One a Day as Traffic Thins

[E-MODERATE] UKMTO said two tankers leaving the Gulf were struck separately by unknown projectiles near Oman on November 21: one on the stern and one in the engine room. No casualties were reported. Iran's navy said a third tanker hit a mine east of the strait and caught fire; that claim is not counted here. (S-010, S-011, S-015)

[E-LOW] UKMTO data counts about 19 vessels targeted from November 1 to 18, against 31 from September 30 to November 9. Its weekly count shows 263 transits from November 12 to 18, down 15 percent, including 37 oil tankers against 52 the week before. One outlet counts at least 25 tankers hit in or near the strait in seven days. (S-013, S-012, S-014)

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: Strikes on shipping now run near one a day and are thinning tanker traffic even as overall flows keep oil prices falling. A merchant vessel struck in or near the strait by November 25 is likely (EF-063-P2). Two days with no reported hit would change this.

7.4 The President Puts Major Strikes After New Year as Talks Stall

[E-LOW] Fox News reported on November 22 that the President is inclined to order military strikes after the new year if no agreement is reached; it gave no direct quotation on timing. On November 20 he said Tehran wants a deal but not one he can accept, and on November 21 he said he intends a permanent deal that prevents an Iranian nuclear weapon. (S-021, S-025, S-023, S-024)

[E-LOW] Gharibabadi said a Qatari delegation held two hours of talks in Tehran on the evening of November 20, giving no details. Araghchi said Iran's seven conditions, including an end to the blockade and to sanctions, must be implemented before Hormuz reopens, and ruled out moving Iran's 60 percent enriched uranium abroad. (S-026, S-027, S-028)

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: Washington is signaling that the next large decision waits for January, which leaves the exchange of single strikes and small salvos in place. A U.S. strike on Iranian territory reported by U.S. officials by December 6 is a roughly even chance (EF-063-P4), since the November 18 and 21 attacks drew no reported U.S. reply. A ceasefire or framework announced by both sides by December 31 remains very unlikely (EF-062-P5). A mediator naming a date for talks would change this.
