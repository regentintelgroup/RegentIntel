7.1 The El Gaia: Two Commands, Two Accounts

[E-MODERATE] CENTCOM stated on September 14 that the Panama-flagged oil tanker El Gaia was struck by an Iranian missile last month and rendered inoperable, that Iran struck it again this weekend with a drone while it sat in waters off Oman, that it is being towed by a regional partner, and that the IRGC claim of a mine strike is false. The IRGC Navy had stated through IRNA that the tanker exploded after hitting a naval mine on a restricted southern route and was fully engulfed (S-004, S-005, S-006).

[E-MODERATE] India's Ministry of External Affairs stated that the vessel carried 14 Indian crew, 13 rescued and one missing; the ship's AIS transponder had been off for more than five days after a call at Fujairah, and it appears on the Persian Gulf Strait Authority's non-compliant list, now published at 57 vessels. Iranian state media had reported one crew member killed and named him; the two casualty accounts are not reconciled (S-008).

[E-LOW] The IRGC separately claimed that its air defense destroyed an MQ-1 drone over the strait; no US statement addressed the claim. No Iranian ballistic launch at a host state was reported for a seventh day and no CENTCOM strike release was published (S-007, S-030).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: Three hypotheses were assessed for the El Gaia strikes: H1, Iran struck the tanker twice, as CENTCOM states, as enforcement against a listed vessel; H2, the vessel struck a mine, as the IRGC states; H3, a US or partner strike within the tanker campaign, as the Qeshm governor asserted on September 13. H1 is least contradicted by available evidence and is carried as the working assessment (likely, moderate confidence): the vessel was on Iran's non-compliant list, had been dark for five days, and the IRGC's own statement describes an illegal passage that its authority had warned would be punished. H2 is assessed unlikely because the vessel was already disabled and stationary off Oman by CENTCOM's account, which a mine strike on a transiting vessel does not fit. H3 is assessed very unlikely given that CENTCOM has named the attacker and the command has not concealed prior tanker strikes. Attribution rung reached: 6 (state direction), as stated by CENTCOM; the evidence is a command statement without published imagery, and the register carries it CONTESTED against the IRGC statement under source symmetry, which caps confidence at moderate. The single piece of evidence that would most change this assessment is imagery of the impact or a UKMTO characterization of the munition. The judgment that the fire pause holds a further 72 hours (KJ-1) is assessed likely with moderate confidence, on the assumption that Tehran holds fire on host states while it contests the El Gaia attribution by messaging rather than by salvo; the pause has now outlived the Salalah date and the strike it appeared to be protecting.

7.2 Riyadh's Air Campaign and the Bab al-Mandeb

[E-MODERATE] The Houthi military spokesman counted 129 Saudi air raids in 48 hours across seven governorates, flown by F-15 and Typhoon aircraft from Khamis Mushait and Taif; Al Jazeera's Sanaa correspondent gave the same count and on September 15 the movement counted a further 54. Saudi Arabia has acknowledged none of them (S-010, S-012).

[E-MODERATE] A Yemeni security source told The National that Houthi forces suffered heavy losses in a declared kill box south of Hodeidah, and the analyst Mohammed al-Basha assessed with high confidence that the movement can no longer exercise effective control over the Bab al-Mandeb and has withdrawn most of its forces (S-010, S-012).

[E-MODERATE] The Saudi-led coalition stated that 13 civilians were injured in Houthi attacks on Khamis Mushait, Abha, and Taif on September 14; the Houthis claimed a missile and drone strike on a base at Sharurah. Government forces have not secured the coast, a Presidential Leadership Council member stated that withdrawn forces are regrouping in Marib, and the Yemeni government banned all Iranian goods. IOM's displacement count passed 85,000 (S-011, S-039, S-025).

[E-MODERATE] Prince Faisal told the BRICS summit that the Kingdom will not accept the targeting of its territory or facilities under any pretext or designation; Egypt's president called on Iran to end attacks on Arab states. The President nominated Representative Wesley Hunt as ambassador to Riyadh, a post vacant since January 2025 (S-018, S-019, S-035).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: Three hypotheses were assessed for the Houthi position on the strait: H1, the movement withdrew most forces from Mayun and the Mocha-Dhubab coast under air attack and holds the ground only nominally; H2, the movement dispersed in place to reduce its signature and retains the islands; H3, the withdrawal is a feint ahead of a push on Marib. H1 is least contradicted by available evidence and is carried as the working assessment (likely, low confidence) because two independent accounts describe withdrawal and the government describes no ground contact. H2 is assessed roughly even chance because the Yemeni source itself describes the movement minimizing visible presence. H3 is assessed unlikely. The single piece of evidence that would most change this assessment is a government or Saudi claim to have landed on Mayun. A Saudi acknowledgement of the campaign (EF-004-P5) is assessed unlikely with low confidence on the assumption that Riyadh keeps its air campaign unacknowledged while the Mecca defense pact is not invoked. 7.3 VTB: The Sanctions Campaign Reaches a Russian Bank

[E-HIGH] OFAC designated VTB Bank on September 14 under Executive Order 13902 for operating in Iran's financial sector, stating that over three years the bank established correspondent relationships with sanctioned Iranian institutions, moved to increase its presence in Tehran from January 2025, took steps to move billions of frozen Iranian assets, and built a rial-ruble settlement system. VTB is Russia's second-largest bank and was already blocked under Russia authorities since 2022 (S-001, S-002).

[E-MODERATE] The designation is the third bank action under Operation Economic Outcast after Banque Misr's Emirati branches and Turkey's Golden Global Bank; the UAE central bank opened an examination of sanctioned branches in the country. A former Treasury official assessed that the action raises secondary-sanctions exposure for Chinese institutions that clear for VTB, the only Russian bank with a branch in China; the 10-year Treasury yield touched 5 percent the same day (S-003, S-040).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: The designation confirms EF-001-P5 and EF-002-P2 and resolves the country question in a direction the market had not priced: Russia, not a Gulf or Turkish counterparty, with China as the next exposed clearing jurisdiction. The practical effect on VTB is limited because the bank was already blocked; the effect is on third-country banks that had treated Russia-program exposure and Iran-program exposure as separable. Three designations in three weeks on a roughly weekly cadence, September 4, the Banque Misr rulemaking, and September 14, is a pattern by this series' count, and the judgment that Treasury designates at least one further non-Iranian institution within 14 days (KJ-4) is assessed likely with moderate confidence on the assumption that the weekly cadence of bank designations since September 4 continues. Whether Moscow answers at the Security Council on September 17 is the linked question; a veto of the panel renewal (EF-004-P2) is assessed roughly even chance with low confidence on the assumption that Moscow answers the VTB designation and the Board referral with a veto rather than an abstention.

7.4 Command Authority in Tehran: The July Attacks Account

[E-LOW] The New York Times reported on September 13, citing multiple unnamed Iranian and IRGC officials, that the July 6-7 attacks on three commercial vessels that collapsed the June memorandum were carried out by hardliners without the authorization of President Pezeshkian, IRGC commander Vahidi, or the Supreme National Security Council; that Iranian investigations traced them to the cleric Hossein Taeb, since appointed to head the Basij; and that Pezeshkian doubts the authenticity of written statements circulated in the Supreme Leader's name (S-009).

[E-MODERATE] The window's Iranian statements are consistent with a divided system: Araghchi stated the Oman arrangement does not reopen the strait absent US compliance with the June memorandum; Baghaei named Saudi Arabia as the party that blocked Salalah; the parliament's presiding board spokesman stated that NPT withdrawal is easily achievable; Mehr called the President's claim of constant Iranian calls absurd (S-027, S-028, S-017, S-015).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: The evidence chain on command authority runs: OBSERVED, a newspaper account of unauthorized July attacks traced to a named cleric; KNOWN, the Supreme Leader has not appeared in public since February 28 and his statements are issued in writing; HISTORICAL, the IRGC Navy has acted ahead of civilian authority in the strait before, in 2019 and 2023; PATTERN, one reported instance in this war, an observation; INFERENCE, at least one attack category in this war did not issue from the state's designated authorities; HIGHER-ORDER INFERENCE, the fire pause and its end may reflect a factional balance rather than a policy. Attribution rung reached for the July attacks: 3 (threat actor association, as reported), because the account rests on anonymous officials. The account is carried at low confidence and does not move the working assessment on retaliation, which continues to treat the foreign ministry's legitimate-defense formulation as the state's stated trigger; it adds Gap 3. Public confirmation of Taeb's removal (EF-004-P13) is assessed very unlikely with low confidence on the assumption that the Supreme Leader's office does not act against the faction the newspaper account names while he remains unseen.
