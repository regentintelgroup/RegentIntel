7.2 Riyadh Closes Its Schools as the Houthis Promise More

[E-MODERATE] Parents across Riyadh received emails on Sunday announcing a shift to remote learning for a week; two messages seen by AFP said Saudi authorities had directed that schools remain closed all week, with no reason given. The coalition said Saturday it intercepted two drones toward the Riyadh area and two ballistic missiles targeting Khamis Mushait; Houthi Political Bureau member Hezam al-Assad called the operation a small-scale rehearsal for a full-scale escalation (S-006, S-019).

[E-MODERATE] The Security Council condemned the Houthi attacks in the strongest terms and demanded they cease. Pakistan's prime minister called further attacks on Mecca a red line; Turkey and Pakistan agreed to chief-of-staff talks with Saudi Arabia; Yemen's government called a general mobilization; Houthi media said a Saudi airstrike on a Taiz marketplace killed seven (S-020, S-022, S-008).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: The Saudi government's own read of the threat to its capital is the strongest indicator on this front: a week-long school closure after one drone pair and one missile pair means Riyadh expects more or knows more. Attribution rung reached: 5 (organizational sponsorship) for the campaign as the Houthis claim it. The Houthis will likely strike Saudi territory again within 72 hours (EF-016-P2); the movement has announced escalation, its condition is unmet, and the Security Council's statement gives it nothing. A Saudi announcement of strikes beyond Yemen, or a Houthi impact inside Riyadh, would move this judgment up, not down. A Houthi suspension statement would move it down.

7.3 Two Measures of the Strait That Cannot Both Be Right

[E-MODERATE] A US official told CBS that nearly 40 million barrels of crude transited the strait under US escort in 48 hours, a number equal to prewar levels; CENTCOM put its redirection count at 122, 12 this week. Kpler's tracking put outflow at 33.7 million barrels for the whole week from September 20, and UKMTO said traffic remains about 75 percent below pre-conflict. Brent closed the week at $104.32 after reaching $108 on Thursday; the White House said the President urged Xi to increase refined product output (S-013, S-014, S-015).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: The official figure and the commercial count are CONTESTED and neither is the baseline until a third measure arrives. Forty million barrels in two days would be five times Kpler's weekly rate; the likeliest reconciliation is that the official figure counts barrels aboard escorted hulls, not barrels delivered past the Gulf of Oman. Brent has a roughly even chance of settling below $100 on at least one day within 14 days (EF-016-P9). Riyadh will likely confirm Yanbu loadings within 14 days (EF-016-P5). A Kpler or Windward weekly count above 50 million barrels would settle the dispute in the official figure's favor.
