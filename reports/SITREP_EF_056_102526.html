3.1 Resumption of Major U.S. Strikes

Definitions used in this product. "Strike": any U.S. munition delivered against Iranian sovereign territory, islands, or territorial waters, confirmed by a CENTCOM release or two independent wire origins. "Campaign": U.S. strikes on Iranian territory on three or more days within any seven-day window, or a CENTCOM-named operation. Strikes on IRGC vessels at sea outside territorial waters do not count as either.

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: We assess a roughly even chance (45 to 55 percent) of at least one U.S. strike on Iranian territory before 0000 EST November 3. We have LOW confidence in this judgment. Conditional on a strike occurring before November 3, we assess it is improbable (20 to 45 percent) that it becomes a campaign before November 3. We have LOW confidence. We assess a roughly even chance (45 to 55 percent) that a campaign occurs between November 4 and December 31 if no signed or de facto U.S.-Iran arrangement exists; we have MODERATE confidence. Because an arrangement by November 30 is itself highly improbable (3.2), the unconditional chance of a campaign by December 31 is also roughly even. The restraint case rests on one item: the President's October 8 written pledge, which carried no condition beyond the date. The escalation case rests on items that all post-date it: the George Washington rearming now with bunker-buster and standoff munitions, the Theodore Roosevelt group (about 6,000) and 13th MEU (about 4,200) ordered to arrive by end of November, reported U.S.-Israeli joint attack planning, the Saudi ordered departure, the DNI warning, and the President's own "maybe tomorrow" and "finish it off" remarks. The in-conflict base rate cuts the other way: three prior signals of resumption since the April ceasefire (MoU expiry in August, the September 26 rejection, mid-October leaks) produced no strike. ([GOV-ALLIED] Truth Social Oct 8 via Al Jazeera Oct 9; [PRESS] Al Jazeera Oct 25 citing The Hill, Axios, NYT; [PRESS] The Week Oct 21; [PRESS] Gulf News citing WSJ Oct 24; [PRESS] Fox News Oct 24; [WIRE-carried] Anadolu Oct 24)

The President's statements are internally inconsistent across 16 days. October 8 [GOV-ALLIED]: "We will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the midterm elections to be held in the United States on November 3rd." October 24 [GOV-ALLIED]: "We're thinking about doing it, and maybe tomorrow." U.S. Ambassador to Turkey Tom Barrack, asked whether a strike could come this weekend: "Absolutely." Two readings are carried with near-equal weight: coercive bargaining tied to the Qatar-Pakistan proposal, and a stated timeline used as cover, which is the June 2025 precedent. The Israeli vote on October 27 is a branch point: the President has said that if Israel chooses "anyone other than Bibi, America will have to do this work ourselves," so an opposition lead on October 28 raises the pre-November 3 strike chance rather than lowering it.

Alternative View: A pre-election strike timed to help Netanyahu, or a strike framed as pre-emption of the plots described in the DNI memo, occurs in the October 26 to 28 window: improbable (20 to 45 percent). The rearming report, the "Absolutely" remark, and the President's open preference for Netanyahu support it. It would become the lead assessment if a further ordered departure is announced for Bahrain, Qatar, Kuwait, or the UAE before October 27, or if CENTCOM reports U.S. ISR activity over Iranian territory (Iran claims a U.S.-developed drone was downed over Bandar Abbas on October 25; unverified).

3.2 Diplomatic Track

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: We assess it is highly improbable (5 to 20 percent) that a signed U.S.-Iran instrument beyond the expired Islamabad MoU exists by November 30. We have MODERATE confidence in this judgment. The base rate is one signed instrument in about eight months of war and ceasefire, and none in about four months of ceasefire across five Munir visits. If decision authority in Tehran is collective (3.5), any instrument requires SNSC consensus, which lengthens the timeline further. Qatar and Pakistan delivered a "middle-ground proposal" in Tehran on October 20 (a secret IAEA visit; U.S. release of interest on $6 billion frozen in Qatar), and anonymous sources say both sides responded positively. Against that: Iran states it will not talk until its "seven conditions" are met and the blockade is lifted first; the Supreme National Security Council secretary says "Not even a tiny bit of enriched nuclear material will leave Iran"; the United States still demands dismantlement; the President rejected an Iranian proposal on September 26; and Iran's chief negotiator withdrew from the BRICS parliamentary forum citing "unforeseen circumstances." ([PRESS] The New Arab Oct 20; [PRESS] The Nation (Pakistan) Oct 21; [PRESS] Al Jazeera Oct 20 and 25)

Alternative View: A narrow, unsigned confidence-building exchange (IAEA visit for interest payments) is concluded quietly before November 30 and is used by both sides to defer strikes: improbable (20 to 45 percent). This matters because it would avert a campaign without producing a signed instrument. Supporting evidence is a single anonymous report of positive responses and the Pezeshkian-Putin meeting on October 27, which gives Tehran a face-saving venue; if that report is wrong, this alternative and the bargaining reading in 3.1 both weaken. It would become the lead assessment if the IAEA confirms access to any Iranian site or if Treasury publishes a license touching the Qatar-held funds.

3.3 Strait of Hormuz

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: We assess it is probable (55 to 80 percent) that UKMTO publishes ten or more attack incidents in the Strait of Hormuz and Gulf of Oman in each of the two reporting weeks ending on or before November 8, counting UKMTO advisories only and excluding IRGC claims. We have MODERATE confidence; UKMTO states it is "struggling to accurately report true number of attacks," so the published count is an undercount. We assess it is probable (55 to 80 percent) that ICE Brent front-month settles above $100 on every trading day through November 6, and highly probable (80 to 95 percent) that it settles above $100 on November 6 itself. We have MODERATE confidence in both. The running UKMTO mean since July 6 is about five projectile incidents per week (79 in about 16 weeks); October is a spike above that mean, with at least 12 attacks in the week ending October 16 (one sunk, two disabled), 254 transits in seven days, and 57 of the 79 incidents on the southern route along Oman's coast. The widely reported figure of "more than 120 attacks in October" is on a different counting basis and includes unverified IRGC claims; it is not used in our threshold. Kpler flows were 11.6 million barrels per day to October 13, about 68 percent of the pre-war 17.1 million. Brent was below $97 at the start of October under the same blockade, so the $100 line is a sentiment-dependent cushion, not a floor. ([TRADE] UKMTO via Al Jazeera Oct 17 and Fox Oct 23; [TRADE] Kpler via Al Jazeera; [TRADE] Seatrade Oct 13; [TRADE] FinanceFeeds citing Reuters and AP Oct 24)

Competing control claims are documented and unresolved. CENTCOM commander Adm. Brad Cooper [GOV-ALLIED], October 16: "Let me be clear: The Strait of Hormuz is controlled by the United States and our regional partners, not Iran." Iran's armed forces chief of staff Ali Abdollahi [ADVERSARY-SOURCED], October 24: U.S. ships will not enter the Gulf "under any circumstances." The IRGC claims [ADVERSARY-SOURCED] seven tankers damaged early in the week, four U.S.-linked tankers struck October 22, and two supertankers carrying 4.5 million barrels mined and abandoned October 23; the Associated Press could not verify these, and they are not counted in our figures.

Alternative View: Tempo drops below ten per week in the fortnight around the U.S. midterms because Iran throttles attacks to deny a strike pretext while the mediator channel is live, then resumes or surges after November 3 or after any U.S. strike: improbable (20 to 45 percent), and this is the same channel we weigh in 3.1 and 3.2, so the two judgments are deliberately not at opposite extremes. Araghchi's October 25 statement that shipping routes "will no longer be the same" also points to a tolling and authorization model that could lower kinetic counts while raising coercion. It would become the lead assessment if UKMTO publishes fewer than five incidents in any seven-day window after October 28.

3.4 Houthi Campaign Against Saudi Arabia and Gulf Energy

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: We assess it is highly probable (80 to 95 percent) that Houthi attacks or coalition-confirmed intercepts over Saudi territory occur in each of the next two weeks through November 8, and nearly certain (95 to 99 percent) that at least one occurs. We have HIGH confidence. We assess it is improbable (20 to 45 percent) that the United States begins direct kinetic strikes on Houthi targets in Yemen within 30 days. We have LOW confidence. The base rate is zero U.S. strikes on the Houthis in 240 days of conflict, and that restraint held through the October 10 mass-casualty strike on Riyadh, the Aramco evacuation, and the ordered departure; if the United States strikes anyone in the next 30 days, Iran proper is the more likely target (3.1). The October 10 attack on King Khalid International Airport killed at least 12 to 13 and wounded more than 300; the attack sequenced drones ahead of cruise and ballistic missiles over 1,000 kilometers, and Saudi Arabia has opened an investigation into why Patriot batteries did not intercept. King Fahd Airport (Dammam) was struck October 21 (2 killed, 8 wounded), a drone was intercepted at the Shaybah oilfield (2 killed), and Aramco ordered its Dhahran headquarters evacuated October 22. The coalition reported ten ballistic missiles intercepted toward southern Saudi Arabia on October 25 and issued civil defense warnings for Riyadh, Khamis Mushait, and Abha. Saudi airstrikes on Sanaa, Taiz, and Mocha continue. The United States provides intelligence and planning but has not struck the Houthis. ([WIRE] AP via PBS Oct 12; [PRESS] Al Jazeera Oct 9, 10, 22; [PRESS] ABC Australia Oct 25; [PRESS] Gulf News Oct 24; [PRESS] Fox Oct 23 citing Reuters)

Alternative View: A Saudi-Houthi pause brokered by Oman or tied to the wider deal track cuts the tempo within two weeks: highly improbable to improbable (15 to 30 percent). Araghchi offered "any possible assistance" toward Saudi-Yemen dialogue on October 11, and Riyadh has reopened the airport. Separately, Saudi Arabia with Pakistani and Turkish forces under the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement may escalate in Yemen without U.S. strikes; Pakistan has said its forces "would not cross the border into Yemen." The pause alternative would become the lead assessment if the coalition reports no intercepts for five consecutive days.

3.5 Iranian Leadership and Decision Authority

Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei has made no public appearance in about seven months since his selection in March. All communications have been written, some in attributed handwriting. Regime outlets report individual meetings with President Pezeshkian (May), Khatam al-Anbiya commander Ali Abdollahi (later), chief negotiator Ghalibaf (the week of October 19), and judiciary chief Mohseni-Ejei (reported October 24), each at an undisclosed time and place with no video. No foreign principal (Munir across five visits, Lavrov on October 19) has publicly claimed to have met him. ([PRESS] Times of Israel Oct 24; [PRESS] Arab News; [PRESS] The Nation (Pakistan) Oct 21)

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: Three hypotheses are carried separately. H1, alive but medically unable to appear: improbable (20 to 45 percent), upper half. H2, able but concealed under an extreme security regime after his father's assassination and with Israel naming regime change as its "primary objective": roughly even (45 to 55 percent). H3, dead or wholly incapacitated with the office exercised collectively behind his name: highly improbable (5 to 20 percent). We have LOW confidence across all three because every item is regime-sourced. H1 and H2 cannot be separated in open sources and the split between them is a characterization, not a prediction; the handwritten messages weigh against H3 and against severe H1. On decision authority: no observable in this cycle shows the Supreme Leader personally directing or overruling the Supreme National Security Council, and messaging is uniform across the SNSC secretary (Rezaei), the chief negotiator (Ghalibaf), the armed forces chief (Abdollahi), and the foreign minister. We characterize decision-making as collective with LOW confidence; public statements by subordinates do not discriminate between a concealed leader who directs in writing and a collective acting in his name.

Alternative View: H2 with full authority: he is healthy and directs policy by written instrument, and the concealment extends even to friendly foreign delegations by design, as other targeted leaders have done for years. Under this hypothesis the absence of video carries little weight and will persist regardless of health. It would become the lead assessment with high confidence if a foreign principal (Putin on October 27, or Field Marshal Munir) states they met him in person, or if any dated live video or audio is released. Ghalibaf's withdrawal from the BRICS forum citing "unforeseen circumstances" is noted and treated as non-diagnostic: it is consistent with a negotiating crunch, strike expectation, or an internal matter.

Section 9: Downgrade and Upgrade Criteria

Downgrade (assessment of imminent escalation revised downward)

  • IAEA publicly confirms access to any Iranian site, or Treasury issues a license touching the $6 billion held in Qatar.
  • A UKMTO seven-day window with fewer than five reported incidents after October 28.
  • The Roosevelt strike group or 13th MEU is publicly redirected away from CENTCOM.
  • A formal White House statement reaffirming no strikes before November 3 without conditions.

Upgrade (assessment revised upward to imminent major escalation)

  • Ordered departure extended to Bahrain, Qatar, Kuwait, or the UAE, or a U.S. Embassy Baghdad evacuation.
  • CENTCOM announces any strike on Iranian territory, or Iran announces a strike on a U.S. base.
  • Confirmed U.S. fatalities from an Iranian or proxy attack at sea or ashore.
  • Confirmed damage to a Gulf undersea cable, or Iranian strike on a Gulf data center.
  • Dated live video or audio of the Supreme Leader authorizing offensive action (removes ambiguity in 3.5 and signals decision closure).
  • Israeli opposition bloc leads on October 28 (raises the pre-November 3 U.S. strike chance on the President's own stated condition).
