7.1 Hengam Island Strike and the Unclaimed Series in the Strait

[E-MODERATE] UKMTO reported at 2300 GMT on September 12 that a vessel transiting the Strait of Hormuz had been struck by an unknown projectile, and later that a fire had broken out aboard and local authorities were evacuating the crew. UKMTO did not identify the vessel, its flag, or its cargo (S-001, S-036).

[E-LOW] Iranian state media stated that an Iranian container ship carrying ten crew was hit at about 0500 local near Hengam Island off the Shib Deraz coast of Qeshm, killing one crew member, named as Jamshid Rajabi, and wounding four. The governor of Qeshm blamed a terrorist enemy and stated that neither the projectile nor its origin had been identified. At least one Iranian account carried the strike as American (S-002, S-040).

[E-MODERATE] No CENTCOM release addressed the strike through the cutoff, a fourth consecutive day without a published command action. The last announced kinetic action remains the five crude carriers struck late on September 8. The two hulls struck west of Khasab on September 11 and the explosions reported at Sirik, Minab, Jask, Qeshm, and Bandar Abbas overnight into September 10 also remain unclaimed (S-003, S-034).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: Three hypotheses were assessed for the Hengam strike: H1, an unannounced US strike within the tanker-for-tanker campaign; H2, an Israeli or other third-party strike within the unexplained Hormozgan series; H3, an Iranian weapon, whether a mine or an IRGC engagement in error. H1 is least contradicted by available evidence and is carried as the working assessment (likely, low confidence). H2 is assessed unlikely. H3 is assessed unlikely given UKMTO's description of a projectile rather than a mine. The single piece of evidence that would most change this assessment is a CENTCOM release or an Iranian armed forces statement naming the weapon. Attribution rung reached: 1 (activity observed). Evidence for rung 3 is not present in available OSINT.

Evidence chain. OBSERVED: a container ship burned near Hengam with one dead (S-001, S-002). INFERENCE: the strike falls inside the campaign's operating area and the campaign's principal has not described it. HIGHER-ORDER INFERENCE: the American silence and the Iranian restraint are both provisional and both tied to the Salalah date.

[E-MODERATE] Gulf and Iranian foreign ministers are scheduled to meet in Salalah on September 14, the first such gathering since February. Bahrain stated on September 12 that it would not attend, citing attacks on infrastructure across the Gulf and calling for Hormuz to be open without discrimination, fees, or permits. The Omani framework under discussion includes voluntary payments for navigational safety, and US officials have told Gulf counterparts they want any US-Iran negotiation refocused on the nuclear program (S-007, S-008).

[E-LOW] President Pezeshkian told India Today that the strait would open on condition the United States ends its blockade. Ebrahim Azizi, head of the parliamentary national security committee, stated that dialogue is useless unless Washington accepts all of Iran's conditions. Pezeshkian separately stated that Iran and the UAE agreed to turn the page after his meeting with the Abu Dhabi crown prince in New Delhi (S-010, S-027, S-028).

CENTCOM stated on September 12 that 100 vessels have been redirected since July 14 and that zero passed without permission. The Financial Times reported that since early September the US has asked tankers to transit at set times to receive protection (S-015, S-019, S-041).
