7.1 The Salalah Postponement and the Gulf Track

[E-HIGH] Oman's foreign minister stated on September 13 that the regional meeting set for September 14 in Salalah had been postponed in the interests of consensus, and the Omani foreign ministry stated that the delay was intended to secure conditions for a constructive dialogue. No new date was given (S-001, S-002).

[E-MODERATE] Bahrain stated on September 12 that it would not attend, citing the absence of diplomatic relations with Tehran and the need for Hormuz to be open without fees or permits. Only Iraq had publicly confirmed attendance. Bloomberg's sources had stated on September 12 that the meeting was unconfirmed and that attendance by all six GCC states was unclear (S-004, S-002).

[E-MODERATE] CNN reported that administration officials had reviewed the Omani plan and that a European diplomat described symbolic new rules as acceptable if they allowed both sides to save face. The President stated the same day that he prefers the current position, with what he called almost total control of the strait (S-043, S-029).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: Three hypotheses were assessed for the postponement: H1, Bahrain's refusal alone made a six-state formula impossible and Muscat chose delay over a five-state meeting; H2, Riyadh or Abu Dhabi withheld attendance after the President's attribution of the pipeline attack to Iran on September 12; H3, Tehran sought the delay to avoid signing a route arrangement while the fire pause and the Treasury designation were unresolved. H1 is least contradicted by available evidence and is carried as the working assessment (likely, low confidence). H2 is assessed roughly even chance given that no Saudi or Emirati statement on attendance was published. H3 is assessed unlikely given the Fars line that regional states requested the delay. The single piece of evidence that would most change this assessment is an Omani or Saudi statement naming the state that asked for postponement. The judgment that a rescheduled ministerial convenes with four or more GCC states within 14 days (KJ-2) is assessed unlikely with low confidence, on the assumption that Bahrain and at least one other GCC state withhold attendance absent a US-Iran understanding.

7.2 The Qeshm Strike and the Fire Pause

[E-MODERATE] The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations issued a verified warning at 2300 UTC on September 12 that a vessel transiting the Strait of Hormuz had been struck by an unknown projectile and that a fire had broken out; it did not identify the vessel or the origin of the projectile. A compilation of Iranian state reporting places the strike near Hengam Island off the Shib Deraz coast of Qeshm at about 0500 local on September 13 (S-006, S-007).

[E-LOW] Iranian state media stated that the vessel carried ten crew, that one crew member was killed and four wounded, and that the governor of Qeshm blamed a terrorist enemy without identifying the weapon or the attacker. One Iranian account carried the strike as American. No US statement addressing the strike was located, and CENTCOM has published no strike release for four days (S-005, S-007, S-016).

[E-MODERATE] No Iranian ballistic launch at a host state was reported for a fifth day. Attacks on Iranian shipping on September 1 and September 8 each produced ballistic salvos at host states within hours under the foreign ministry's stated formulation of self-defense; that formulation was not invoked in this window. Explosions along the Hormozgan coast overnight into September 10 and two hulls struck west of Khasab on September 11 remain unclaimed by any party (S-016, S-037).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: Three hypotheses were assessed for the Qeshm strike: H1, a US strike within the tanker campaign; H2, a strike within the unattributed Hormozgan series of September 10 and 11, by an actor other than the United States; H3, an Iranian mine or misfire. H2 is least contradicted by available evidence and is carried as the working assessment (roughly even chance, low confidence), because the vessel was a cargo ship rather than a crude carrier, CENTCOM has announced only tanker strikes, and the location matches the unattributed series. H1 is assessed roughly even chance given that no US statement has denied it. H3 is assessed unlikely given the projectile description from UKMTO. Attribution rung reached: 1 (activity observed). Evidence for any higher rung is not present in available OSINT. The single piece of evidence that would most change this assessment is a CENTCOM release claiming the vessel. The judgment that the fire pause holds a further 72 hours (KJ-1) is assessed likely with moderate confidence, on the assumption that Tehran continues to hold fire while a rescheduled regional meeting remains in prospect.

7.3 Pipeline Attribution: Baghdad's Launcher and Washington's Statement

[E-MODERATE] Iraq's Security Media Cell stated on September 12 that a task force had located and seized the drone-launching platform used against the Saudi East-West pipeline, that forensic work to identify those responsible continues, and that the commander-in-chief approved an Iranian request to join the investigation. Baghdad closed and partially reopened three crossings with Iran and dismissed the Maysan operations commander and provincial police chief (S-011).

[E-MODERATE] Al Jazeera's correspondent placed the launch area in the al-Tayeb district of northeastern Maysan, about 1,100 kilometers from Medina and 800 kilometers from Riyadh, distances that imply long-range rather than tactical drones. An umbrella body of Iran-aligned Iraqi militias denied involvement; neither Riyadh nor Baghdad has accused any state (S-012, S-038).

[E-MODERATE] The President, asked in Dublin on September 12 whether Iran was behind the attack, stated that it probably was. This is the first attribution to a state by any government and it conflicts with the Saudi and Iraqi positions; the register carries the presidential statement as CONTESTED against those positions, under the same standard applied to Iranian claims. No Iranian statement on the attack or on the attribution was located (S-008, S-042).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: The evidence chain on Iranian responsibility runs: OBSERVED, drones launched from al-Tayeb, Maysan, struck the pipeline at ranges of 800 to 1,100 kilometers; KNOWN, Iraqi factions have used short-range systems against the Kurdistan Region, and long-range systems require external supply; HISTORICAL, Iran has supplied long-range drones to Iraqi factions since 2019; PATTERN, one launch event, which is an observation and not a pattern; INFERENCE, the launching formation used Iranian-supplied systems; HIGHER-ORDER INFERENCE, the attack served the Iranian campaign against the blockade whether or not Tehran ordered it. Attribution rung reached: 1 (activity observed, launch origin fixed to Maysan). The presidential statement does not raise the rung because it cites no evidence. The prior working hypothesis, that an Iraqi militia launched on its own decision within a shared campaign, stands (likely, low confidence). Baghdad's admission of Iranian investigators lowers the probability of a public naming of the formation; that judgment (EF-002-P9) is assessed unlikely with low confidence on the assumption that Baghdad's investigation with Iranian participation does not produce a public attribution.
