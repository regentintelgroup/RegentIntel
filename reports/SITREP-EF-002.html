7.1 Hormuz: The Hengam Island Strike and the Fire Pause

[E-HIGH] United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations reported early September 13 that a vessel transiting the Strait of Hormuz was struck by an unknown projectile, that a fire broke out, and that local authorities evacuated the crew. Iranian state media placed the strike near Hengam Island off the Shib Deraz coast of Qeshm at about 0500 local, aboard a commercial vessel with ten crew; one crew member was killed and four wounded (S-001, S-002).

[E-LOW] The governor of Qeshm attributed the strike to a terrorist enemy without naming a weapon or an attacker, and at least one Iranian account carried it as an American attack. No US statement addressing the incident was located through the cutoff (S-003, S-012).

[E-MODERATE] No Iranian ballistic salvo at a Gulf host state or Jordan was reported for a fourth consecutive day, the longest interval since attacks resumed August 28. On September 1 and September 8 attacks on Iranian shipping produced host-state salvos within hours; this time no armed forces statement followed. The two hulls struck west of Khasab on September 11 remain unidentified and unclaimed (S-013, S-040).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: Three hypotheses were assessed for the Hengam Island strike: H1, a US strike within the tanker campaign against an Iranian hull; H2, an Iranian mine or shore-launched weapon that struck a friendly hull; H3, an Israeli or unknown third-party strike. H1 is least contradicted by available evidence and is carried as the working assessment (roughly even chance, low confidence). H2 is assessed unlikely. H3 is assessed unlikely given the absence of any Israeli operation reported in the theater over the holiday. The single piece of evidence that would most change this assessment is a CENTCOM release naming the hull. Attribution rung reached: 1 (activity observed). Evidence for rung 5 is not present in available OSINT. Prediction EF-002-P3 (KJ-3), no Iranian launch at a host state through 161600Z SEP 2026, is assessed likely (moderate confidence); the assumption is that the strike is not answered with host-state fire while the Salalah track is live.

7.2 Petroline: Washington Names Iran, Baghdad Produces the Launcher

[E-HIGH] Asked in Dublin on September 12 whether Iran was behind the drone attack that shut the East-West pipeline, the President said he thought it was, adding that it probably was. Saudi Arabia has named Iraq as the point of origin and no state; Iraq has confirmed Maysan province as the launch area and named no group (S-004, S-005, S-009).

[E-MODERATE] Iraq's Security Media Cell stated that a task force located and seized the drone-launching platform, that forensic work to identify those responsible continues, and that the commander-in-chief approved an Iranian request to join the investigation. Al Jazeera placed the launch site in the al-Tayeb district of northeastern Maysan, about 800 kilometers from Riyadh and 1,100 kilometers from Medina, distances its correspondent said imply long-range systems. The Maysan operations commander and police chief were dismissed and three border crossings closed for inspection (S-007, S-008, S-009).

[E-LOW] An umbrella group of Iran-backed Iraqi militias denied involvement. Saudi energy ministry sources place the loss from pumping station damage at about 700,000 barrels per day; no restart date has been announced (S-010, S-019).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: The attribution picture moved in opposite directions in one day. The government whose pipeline was struck named a country of origin; the government from whose soil the drones flew recovered the launcher and named nobody; the government running the blockade named a state. Attribution rung reached: 1 (activity observed, launch origin fixed to al-Tayeb district). Evidence for rung 6 is not present in available OSINT; the presidential statement is a claim, not evidence. The EF-001 working hypothesis, an Iraqi militia launching on its own decision within a shared campaign, is retained (likely, low confidence). The distances reported, if confirmed, narrow the candidate set to formations that hold long-range one-way attack systems.
