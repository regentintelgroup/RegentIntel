SECTION 1: BLUF

The dominant Homeland development of the reporting period is the issuance of a federal law enforcement bulletin in advance of the 25th anniversary of the September 11 attacks, warning that [E-HIGH] foreign terrorist organizations "continue to encourage attacks in the United States and have released 9/11-related propaganda in advance of previous anniversaries of the 9/11 attacks." The bulletin states the FBI is concerned that terrorism actors in the U.S. may be inspired to conduct violence or engage in hoax threats in response to the anniversary, and that those actors have shown willingness to act with little to no warning. Concurrently, the State Department on September 10 tied Kata'ib Hizballah directly to Homeland attack planning, stating that KH "has intensified its threat to U.S. interests, including a recent plot to attack Jewish institutions in the United States." The protective implication is specific and immediate: security directors should posture for low-capability, no-warning lone offender action against high-visibility commemorative gatherings and Jewish institutional targets across the next 72 hours, and should treat hoax threat volume as an operational resource-drain vector rather than a nuisance [E-HIGH / P-MODERATE]. Regent Intel Group assesses that the currently visible Iranian-directed Homeland threat reporting reflects prosecutorial and sanctions disclosure of spring 2026 activity rather than confirmed new tasking, a distinction that materially changes the required posture and is developed in Section 5 [E-MODERATE / P-MODERATE].

SECTION 3: STRATEGIC OVERVIEW

3.1 Anniversary Threat Messaging and Federal Advisory Posture

A federal bulletin was issued to law enforcement partners in the 24 hours preceding the anniversary [E-HIGH]. The document states that messaging by foreign terrorist organizations "in combination with ongoing conflicts in the Middle East could heighten existing grievances against the United States and contribute to mobilization to violence." This is the single most analytically significant line in the reporting period because it establishes an explicit federal linkage between the Operation Epic Fury theater conflict and Homeland mobilization risk, which is the central structural premise of this product line.

A DHS spokesperson characterized the environment in parallel, stating that the United States faces "a heightened and increasingly complex terrorism threat environment," and that the environment has "changed significantly since the attacks of September 11, 2001." The spokesperson noted that individuals and small cells now plot attacks independently, that ISIS and al-Qaeda continue to seek ways to carry out or inspire attacks in the U.S., and that domestic terrorists continue to plan acts of violence often driven by anti-government views. The same official flagged two capability trendlines: that terrorists are increasingly experimenting with generative AI, not only to create and circulate violent online propaganda but to explore ways to use explosives and biological agents, that AI advances could make terrorist activity faster, more scalable, and harder to detect, and that law enforcement is also concerned about the use of drones.

[GOV -- ALLIED] Source symmetry note. The DHS spokesperson characterization above is an official statement assessed against observable evidence, not accepted at face value. Regent Intel Group notes a partial tension between the "heightened and increasingly complex" framing and a separately reported DHS Intelligence and Analysis judgment that no specific credible threat has been identified. This tension is not evidence of bad faith. It is the normal distinction between environmental threat and specific threat. It is documented here because the two statements are being consumed by the public as a single message, and protective decisions should be keyed to the more precise of the two [E-MODERATE].

On the specific-threat question, a practitioner-oriented analytical outlet reports that the DHS Office of Intelligence and Analysis is aware of no specific, credible foreign terrorist or Domestic Violent Extremist threat to events associated with the 25th anniversary of the September 11 attacks [E-LOW]. The same reporting notes that no specific credible threat has been identified, and special events with large attendance and amplified media coverage remain attractive to physical, cyber, and hoax threat actors because of that coverage, and that DHS I&A assesses lone offenders and small groups, radicalized online and operating with minimal coordination, as the most significant terrorism threat in the homeland. This claim is carried at E-LOW because it rests on a single non-wire source characterizing a document Regent Intel Group has not independently reviewed. It is nonetheless consistent with the FBI bulletin reporting and with the DHS spokesperson framing, and the convergence is the reason it is carried at all.

3.2 Iranian State-Sponsored Activity Targeting the Homeland

On September 10, the State Department announced a designations tranche under Operation Economic Outcast targeting Kata'ib Hizballah, Hizballah, and the broader financial networks that sustain Iran's campaign of regional destabilization [E-HIGH] [GOV]. The release states that KH, designated a Foreign Terrorist Organization in 2009, has intensified its threat to U.S. interests, including a recent plot to attack Jewish institutions in the United States, and continues to direct attacks against U.S. and Coalition forces in Iraq.

The underlying Homeland case is the May 2026 prosecution of a senior KH figure. The Justice Department announced the arrest of Mohammad Baqer Saad Dawood Al-Saadi, an Iraqi national and senior member of Kata'ib Hizballah, charged by complaint with six counts of terrorism-related offenses for his activities as an operative of KH and Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps, including his involvement in nearly 20 attacks and attempted attacks throughout Europe and the United States. In or about April and May 2026, Al-Saadi, on behalf of KH and the IRGC, allegedly attempted to coordinate and carry out terrorist attacks in the United States, including by targeting Jewish institutions in New York; on or about April 3, 2026, he allegedly texted an undercover law enforcement officer photographs and maps showing the exact location of a prominent Jewish synagogue in New York, as well as two additional U.S.-based Jewish institutions in Los Angeles, California, and Scottsdale, Arizona, and directed that officer to carry out attacks against those targets. Methods discussed allegedly included arson and the use of improvised explosive devices.

Two network characteristics from this case carry forward as established baseline for this series. First, the front-organization model: federal prosecutors concluded that Harakat Ashab al-Yamin al-Islamia, a newly recognized Iran-aligned group established in March 2026 and attributed to European plots, is essentially a front for KH [E-MODERATE] [RESEARCH]. Second, and of greater protective significance, the outsourcing model: the complaint alleges Al-Saadi provided an undercover law enforcement officer, who was posing as a Mexican cartel member, with photographs and maps showing the locations of his targets [E-HIGH]. This is the TCO nexus in its most operationally relevant form and is developed in Section 5.

3.3 Critical Infrastructure and Cyber-Physical Convergence

The governing federal technical advisory remains in force [E-HIGH] [GOV]. The FBI observed Iranian-affiliated APT actors targeting internet-exposed programmable logic controllers with intent to cause disruption, including maliciously interacting with project files and manipulating data displayed on HMI and SCADA displays, at U.S. critical infrastructure organizations, and assessed that Iranian-affiliated APT targeting campaigns against U.S. critical infrastructure have recently escalated, likely in response to hostilities between Iran, and the United States and Israel. The advisory documents a cyber-physical safety consequence, not merely an IT compromise: at one U.S. victim the FBI observed actors download a malicious project file to a targeted PLC, and analysis indicated the file retained ladder logic for downstream function but added logic that overrode specific instruction sets responsible for maintaining safe operating parameters.

The advisory was originally published April 7, 2026, and updated July 22, 2026, to add guidance on detecting malicious changes in reusable code modules within Rockwell Automation PLC programs, and to expand the manufacturer scope to include observed targeting of Schneider Electric, Siemens, and potentially other branded PLCs. Sectors named include Government Services and Facilities, to include local municipalities, and Water and Wastewater Systems. Detection guidance includes examining logs for suspicious traffic on ports associated with OT devices, including 44818, 2222, 102, and 502, especially traffic originating from overseas hosting providers.

Analytical caution. Independent analysis notes that Iranian-affiliated infrastructure activity has historically used low-sophistication methods that exploit basic vulnerabilities in internet-connected systems, and that the associated persona "CyberAv3ngers," presenting as hacktivist but assessed as an APT associated with the IRGC Cyber Electronic Command, has a history of over-inflating its attacks and their impact, with sowing discord as a primary objective [E-MODERATE] [RESEARCH]. Consequence inflation by the adversary is itself a threat vector. Security directors should expect claimed compromises to outnumber real ones and should pre-plan a verification path before responding to a public claim.

3.4 Aviation, Transportation, and the Counter-UAS Gap

The most concrete transportation security event of the preceding cycle was a drone-driven ground stop at a major U.S. airport [E-MODERATE] [PRESS/TRADE]. Multiple drones were spotted on September 4 in the immediate vicinity of San Diego International Airport beginning at approximately 2:00 p.m. local time, and an airport public information officer confirmed that a drone and a weather balloon were observed near the airport, prompting the FAA to pause all arrivals and departures. The consequential finding is attributional, not operational: the TSA cannot identify the operator because no U.S. commercial airport monitors FAA Remote ID broadcasts in real time, leaving investigators with no sensor logs, no trajectory, and no operator location, and the SAFER SKIES Act implementing rules published in July 2026 address what law enforcement can do with a drone threat, but only if they know one is there.

Federal counter-UAS capability is demonstrably deployable when a venue is designated. TSA's Federal Air Marshal Service, U.S. Secret Service, FBI and CBP reminded the public that drones were restricted during the Republican National Committee's Midterm Convention on September 9 and 10, with a Temporary Flight Restriction established for the duration and the FAA restricting drone flights within a 3-mile radius up to 400 feet, building on DHS efforts at the FIFA World Cup 2026. A FAMS, FBI Kansas City, and Kansas City Police Department counter-UAS joint operation intercepted eight drones in a single day during FIFA World Cup 2026 events. The analytic point for protective planning is the delta between designated-event coverage and routine-day coverage, which is wide.

3.5 Domestic Violent Extremism and the Federal Facility Vector

Federal characterization of the primary domestic threat remains lone offender and small cell action. Independent practitioner reporting on the anniversary reinforces that lone actors and small cells who radicalize online have posed the greatest terrorism threat to the homeland in recent years precisely because they can be so difficult to detect and disrupt, and that many recent attackers or alleged plotters had not previously come onto the radar of the FBI or local law enforcement [E-MODERATE] [PRESS].

The anti-government and anti-immigration-enforcement strand remains the most lethally demonstrated domestic vector in the current period. In the September 24, 2025 Dallas attack, a sniper fired dozens of rounds at an ICE field office from a nearby rooftop, targeting ICE agents and firing indiscriminately; the FBI released a photo of ammunition marked "anti-ICE," officials said the shooter had been planning the attack for months and used a phone app to track agents, and two detainees were killed and another injured. [E-HIGH] Regent Intel Group notes the approach of the one-year anniversary of that attack inside the 14-day outlook window. Enforcement friction has continued through 2026: federal immigration officers shot 14 people from September 2025 to February 2026 as DHS ramped up deportation operations around the country [E-MODERATE] [PRESS], a dynamic that sustains grievance narratives on which the lone offender vector feeds.

3.6 ISIS and al-Qaeda Inspired Activity, and the TCO Nexus

Officials cited in the anniversary reporting state that in the past year and a half the FBI has arrested at least 16 people inside the U.S. homeland for allegedly plotting or supporting plots to kill Americans on behalf of al-Qaeda or ISIS, and that a March U.S. intelligence community worldwide threat report noted that while ISIS and al-Qaeda may be largely relying on social media to reach America, their numbers are growing overseas, with ISIS at up to 18,000 members worldwide and al-Qaeda's ranks reaching up to 28,000 [E-MODERATE] [PRESS reporting GOV figures].

On the immigration and counterterrorism intersection, DHS announced on September 10 that ICE has removed more than 2,000 known or suspected terrorists from the United States, and that from January 20, 2025 to September 9, 2026, ICE arrested more than 2,150 KSTs in the United States and deported 2,015 [E-HIGH] [GOV -- ALLIED].

[GOV -- ALLIED] evidentiary treatment. The KST figures above are an official self-reported enforcement metric released on the eve of a symbolic anniversary. Regent Intel Group applies the same standard here that it applies to adversary claims. The "known or suspected terrorist" category is a watchlisting status, not an adjudicated finding, and the release does not disaggregate by threat category, derogatory basis, or country. The number is therefore reported as an accurate statement of what DHS said, and is not treated as a measure of Homeland threat reduction [E-HIGH for the statement / P-LOW for any inference about residual threat]. DHS separately cited a specific case, describing an individual arrested in June as the suspected ringleader of an alleged mass-casualty plot against a White House event, which if substantiated in court would represent a significant TCO-adjacent Homeland plot.
