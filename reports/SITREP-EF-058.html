A U.S. official called it defensive and not a return to major combat. The President said late on November 6 that U.S. forces had destroyed a small air-defense system on Qeshm within minutes and called the operation minor. (S-001, S-002, S-005, S-048)

[E-MODERATE] The President met his national security team on November 5 and 6 and said a decision would come soon. Axios reported three options: intensified attacks, continued economic pressure, or a settlement with U.S. concessions. CENTCOM is finalizing preparations for major operations but has no new directive. Officials cited the November 9 new moon as favorable. The carrier Roosevelt began a Singapore port call on November 6. (S-013, S-014, S-021, S-022)

[E-MODERATE] Republicans lost heavily on November 3; about six in ten voters in the AP poll called the war the wrong decision. Netanyahu urged the President to resume strikes and got no commitment. (S-024, S-025, S-031)

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: Washington is running two tracks at once: limited strikes at Qeshm, framed as force protection, and an open decision on a campaign. 7.2 Tehran Sent a Proposal but Kept Blockade Relief as the First Step

[E-MODERATE] Iranian negotiators left Doha on November 1 with a draft circulating and a follow-up meeting possible. Foreign ministry spokesman Baghaei said the talks rest on Iran's seven-step plan and that no agreement or Hormuz reopening can begin until the blockade ends. Qatar drafted a two-day sequence: a blockade lift, partial release of frozen funds, and an IAEA invitation on day one; a Hormuz reopening and uranium talks on day two. Neither side accepted it. (S-027, S-021)

[E-MODERATE] Reuters reported on November 5 that Iran sent a formal nuclear proposal through Qatar and that talks depend on the United States meeting seven demands; Washington is reviewing it. Security council secretary Rezaei met President Putin the same day, carrying a message from the Supreme Leader, and Putin offered to mediate. (S-016, S-037, S-023)

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: Tehran has moved on form, not on sequence: a written proposal and a faster timetable still put blockade relief before any uranium commitment. The President's weaker domestic position raises the value of a quick confidence step, but his stated price is the stockpile. A joint announcement of a confidence-building step by December 7 is unlikely (EF-058-P9). The observable that would change this: Iran accepting uranium talks before blockade relief, on the record.
