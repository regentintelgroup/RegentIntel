9.1 First Alien Terrorist Removal Court Removal: FTO-Inspired Vector

[E-HIGH] DOJ announced on September 11 that Nazira Haji Zada, 47, an Afghan national of Fort Worth, Texas, was removed from the United States in the first case ever brought before the Alien Terrorist Removal Court, established by Congress in 1996 and never used by any prior administration. The application was filed July 15, she appeared July 30, the removal order was signed August 20 and unsealed September 11 after her return to Afghanistan (S-001, S-003).

[E-HIGH] Per court documents, Zada supported the plot for which her son Abdullah Haji Zada and son-in-law Nasir Ahmad Tawhedi were arrested on October 7, 2024, after buying two rifles, 500 rounds, and 10 magazines from an undercover FBI employee for an ISIS-inspired mass shooting on Election Day 2024. The family sold property and bought one-way tickets to Kabul. Abdullah was sentenced to 15 years; Tawhedi pleaded guilty and awaits sentencing. The government used classified information and disclosed about half a terabyte of documents to the defense (S-002, S-003).

Attribution rung reached for the 2024 plot: 4 (organizational association, ISIS-inspired), as stated in DOJ charging and plea documents; DOJ did not allege ISIS direction. The removal is a court outcome, not a new plot, and it moves no rating. Its significance for the FTO-inspired vector is procedural: a resident alien tied to a family plot was removed on a preponderance standard with classified evidence, which is a channel JTTFs can now expect to see used again (S-001, S-003).

This is a historic and vitally important step in our work to protect the U.S. homeland from terrorism.
