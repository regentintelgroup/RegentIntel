7.1 The Boats: The Command Confirms It Fired

[E-MODERATE] CENTCOM spokesman Capt. Tim Hawkins stated on September 15 that Iranian small boats recently attempted to take possession of a US unmanned surface vessel, that they were unsuccessful after the command forcefully responded, and that the surface drone remains under US operational control. US officials told Axios that an American drone fired two missiles at two IRGC boats on September 14, destroying them and killing most of those aboard, and that the military initially did not disclose the strike (S-002).

[E-MODERATE] Hawkins told AP the vessel involved was a Saildrone, a craft of about 25 feet, and that all of the command's surface drones remain fully accounted for. Iranian media reported two fishing boats struck by a drone off Kargan and near Larak Island in Hormozgan province, and the deputy governor for security affairs, Ahmad Nafisi, attributed the strike to the United States and stated that fishermen were missing. Neither side has published a casualty figure (S-003, S-004).

[E-MODERATE] The command separately labelled the IRGC Navy's account of the El Gaia false, restating that an Iranian missile disabled the tanker last month and an Iranian drone struck it again off Oman. Oman's Maritime Security Center stated the tanker is under tow with the fire controlled, that a Royal Navy of Oman vessel evacuated 23 crew, that two remain missing, and that the engine-room fire followed an earlier attack; Muscat assigned no responsibility (S-001, S-005).

[E-LOW] The IRGC claimed three MQ-1 shootdowns over or west of the strait in 24 hours. No US confirmation, denial, or wreckage was located; the MQ-1 left Air Force service in 2018. Iranian media reported two explosions heard on Qeshm Island from the direction of the sea at about 0130 local on September 16, unexplained by any party (S-006).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: Three hypotheses were assessed for the September 14 engagement: H1, IRGC boats attempted to seize a Saildrone and were destroyed from the air, as the command and US officials state; H2, the boats were fishermen struck in error, as Iranian media state; H3, the boats were IRGC craft on a different mission, engaged under a broad self-defense authority. H1 is least contradicted by available evidence and is carried as the working assessment (likely, moderate confidence): the command confirmed a seizure attempt, the IRGC had claimed on September 11 to have struck a Saildrone, and the Iranian account supplies no reason for fishing boats to close on a US vessel. H2 is assessed unlikely because the provincial official attributed the strike without describing what the boats were doing. H3 is assessed unlikely. Attribution rung reached for the boats' identity: 5 (state sponsorship, as stated by US officials); the IRGC has not claimed the crews and the register carries the two accounts as an adversary claim against a corroborated official account. The consequential judgment is Iranian: the system described its dead as fishermen rather than avenging them as Guards, which is the response of a state deferring the exchange it would otherwise owe. The judgment that the fire pause holds a further 72 hours (KJ-1) is assessed likely with moderate confidence, on the assumption that Tehran describes its dead at sea as fishermen rather than avenging them as Guards while the Oman channel is open. The single piece of evidence that would most change this assessment is an Iranian armed forces statement naming the boat crews as Guards.

[E-LOW] Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree denied any threat to Mecca and stated on September 16 that the movement fired dozens of ballistic missiles and drones at an Aramco facility in Yanbu and ballistic missiles at Khamis Mushait air base, in response to what he counted as over 450 Saudi air strikes this week; he also claimed a Saudi F-15 downed over Marib. No Saudi confirmation of damage at Yanbu or of any aircraft loss was located (S-007, S-008, S-012).

[E-MODERATE] Al-Malki confirmed 13 civilians injured and seven houses and two vehicles damaged at Khamis Mushait, Abha, and Taif on September 14. Cooper, affirmed the Kingdom's right to self-defense; the Crown Prince flew to Cairo, where Egypt described Saudi security as part of its own. IOM counted 93,864 newly displaced in Yemen (S-011, S-048, S-031).

[E-LOW] Axios reported that Israel and Saudi Arabia, with CENTCOM assistance, are discussing Israeli intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance support for Saudi operations against the Houthis, modeled on Israel's support to the UAE, and that Israeli offensive action is unlikely. Sources told Deccan Chronicle that US officials met Houthi representatives in Oman and that the movement promised not to attack American or Israeli ships (S-018, S-046).

7.3 The Red Sea Route Closes and the Market Prices It

[E-MODERATE] Two sources told ABC News on September 15 that crude loadings at Yanbu have stopped. Reuters reported that Riyadh informed European customers that some late-September cargoes are cancelled, that no Saudi crude has departed Yanbu since September 11 per Vortexa, and that Saudi Arabia could exhaust crude available for export within days unless the East-West pipeline resumes. Aramco declined to comment to Reuters (S-013, S-014).

[E-MODERATE] Brent for November settled at $108.75 on September 15, up $3.07 or 2.9 percent, and WTI at $105.83, the highest closes in nearly four months; dated Brent was quoted above $122 and diesel futures settled at a record. AAA's diesel average reached $6.27 a gallon and regular gasoline $4.32. Goldman Sachs stated the Saudi attacks raise the probability of its scenario in which Brent exceeds $120 (S-015, S-032, S-035).

[E-MODERATE] In Wednesday trade before the cutoff Brent eased to $107.53 at 0655Z and WTI to $104.19 after the American Petroleum Institute reported a 7.1 million barrel build in US crude stocks against an expected draw. Sources told Reuters on September 16 that Saudi Arabia is offering Asian refiners additional crude by ship-to-ship transfer off Sohar, Oman, a third outlet alongside the escorted Hormuz transits (S-050, S-051).

[E-LOW] Return-to-service estimates for the pipeline diverge: the Energy Secretary stated it will be measured in days; Reuters sources stated five to six weeks, with partial flow possible sooner; two regional officials told AP three to five weeks. Aramco and the Ministry of Energy have published no damage assessment, volume, or date (S-029).

[E-MODERATE] A US official told Axios that an average of 40 ships a day transit Hormuz under US guidance carrying roughly 14 million barrels of oil, that daytime transits have begun, and that Saudi Arabia has increased tankers sent through the strait. Reuters and Kpler recorded four commodity vessels on September 15, none a crude or LNG carrier, against a ten-day average of 18. Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority expanded its non-compliant list by 20 to 77 hulls (S-017, S-016, S-030).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: Three hypotheses were assessed for the Yanbu suspension: H1, the terminal is closed by supply, because the pipeline has delivered nothing since September 10 and stock is exhausted; H2, the terminal is closed by risk, because Riyadh will not load with Houthi fire reaching Yanbu; H3, both, with supply the binding constraint. H3 is least contradicted by available evidence and is carried as the working assessment (likely, low confidence): the Reuters chain describes exhaustion of exportable crude, and civil defense alerts for Yanbu on September 15 describe the risk. H1 alone is assessed roughly even chance and H2 alone unlikely, because loadings stopped on September 11, before the first Houthi claim on the terminal. The judgment that Aramco does not announce resumed Yanbu loadings within 14 days (KJ-4) is assessed likely with low confidence, on the assumption that pipeline repair takes weeks rather than days, as Reuters and AP sources state, not the Energy Secretary; confidence is low because the operator has published nothing and the estimates span an order of magnitude. Brent above $105 on every trading day through September 18 (EF-005-P6) is assessed likely with moderate confidence.
