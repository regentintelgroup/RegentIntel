7.1 The Small-Boat Engagement and the Seventh Day of the Fire Pause

[E-HIGH] CENTCOM spokesman Capt. Tim Hawkins stated on September 15 that Iranian small boats attempted to take possession of a US unmanned surface vessel, that they failed after the command forcefully responded, and that the drone remains under US control. AP carried the command's statement that two small boats were destroyed. It is the command's first announced kinetic action since the five crude carriers struck on September 8 (S-002, S-004).

[E-MODERATE] US officials told Axios that an American drone fired two missiles at the boats off Hormozgan on September 14, destroying them and killing most of those aboard, and that the military did not initially disclose the strike. No casualty figure has been published by either government (S-005).

[E-LOW] Iranian media reported the same event as a drone attack on two fishing vessels off Kargan and near Larak Island with several fishermen missing, and the Hormozgan deputy governor attributed the strike to the United States. Iranian media also reported two explosions heard on Qeshm Island from the direction of the sea at about 0130 local on September 16, which no party has explained (S-006, S-034).

[E-HIGH] No Iranian ballistic missile launch at Jordan, Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, the UAE, or Saudi Arabia was reported between the cutoffs, a seventh consecutive day and the longest interval since attacks resumed on August 28. Iran did fire five missiles into Sulaimani province on September 14 and a one-way attack drone at a KDPI base at Balisan on September 15, against Iranian Kurdish opposition positions, without casualties (S-001, S-023).

7.2 Red Sea Closure: Yanbu Suspended, Mecca Alerted

[E-HIGH] Reuters reported and two sources told ABC News that crude loadings at Yanbu are suspended, that no Saudi crude has departed the terminal since September 11 per Vortexa, and that Riyadh told European customers some late-September cargoes are cancelled. Saudi Aramco declined comment to Reuters. Rystad put the export cushion at five to seven days and the loss at 3 million barrels per day of Yanbu loadings (S-007, S-008, S-040).

[E-MODERATE] Estimates of the East-West pipeline outage diverge by an order of magnitude: Energy Secretary Wright told CNBC the return will be measured in days; Reuters sources said repairs could take five to six weeks; two regional officials told AP three to five weeks with partial operation sooner. Saudi Aramco and the Ministry of Energy have published no damage assessment, volume, or date, a fifth day (S-032).

[E-HIGH] The Saudi-led coalition stated that Royal Saudi Air Defense Forces intercepted a Houthi drone south of Mecca at 1850 local on September 15, the first attempt on the city since July 2017, and called the security of the Two Holy Mosques a red line. The coalition confirmed 13 civilians wounded at Khamis Mushait, Abha, and Taif. The Houthis denied any threat to the holy sites and claimed a Saudi F-15 downed over Marib, a claim Riyadh has not addressed (S-003, S-022).

[E-MODERATE] Axios reported that Adm. Cooper hosted the military chiefs of Israel, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Bahrain, Kuwait, Qatar, Jordan, and Egypt in Germany last week and that Israel and Saudi Arabia are discussing Israeli ISR support against the Houthis with CENTCOM's help. CENTCOM confirmed a conference with senior leaders of eight nations, and the Saudi cabinet was briefed on the Crown Prince's meeting with Cooper (S-035, S-036).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: The pipeline outage converts a Houthi-Saudi exchange into a global supply event. With Yanbu idle and the Bab al-Mandeb coast under Houthi control, Saudi export volume has one exit, the Strait of Hormuz under US escort, which returns the Kingdom's barrels to the waterway the war is about. The judgment that Saudi Aramco announces the pipeline back at 5 million barrels per day or more by 301600Z (EF-005-P8, KJ-4) is assessed unlikely with low confidence, on the assumption that physical damage at the Riyadh and Madinah region strike sites requires the multi-week repair that Reuters and AP sources describe; confidence is low because the operator has published nothing and the Energy Secretary's estimate is the shortest on record. The judgment that Houthi forces claim or conduct at least one further attack on Saudi territory by 191600Z (EF-005-P3) is assessed very likely with moderate confidence: the movement has fired at the Kingdom on each of the past four days and Saudi strikes on Yemen continue at 54 raids a day by the Houthi count. The Mecca attempt raises the probability of a Saudi ground or coastal operation and of Israeli ISR support, both of which would widen the war beyond the Iran-US axis. The single indicator that would most change this assessment is an Aramco volume statement.

7.3 Hormuz: Four Visible Transits, Forty Claimed, and the Council Vote

[E-HIGH] Reuters and Kpler recorded four commodity vessels transiting the strait on September 15, two in and two out, none a crude or LNG carrier, against a revised seven on September 14 and a ten-day average of 18. The Joint Maritime Information Center rates the threat SEVERE and counted three vessels attacked in the strait over 72 hours. CENTCOM's blockade tally rose to 103 vessels redirected (S-010, S-011, S-013).

[E-MODERATE] A US official told Axios that an average of 40 ships a day transit under US guidance and protection carrying roughly 14 million barrels of oil, that daytime transits have begun, and that the mine-cleared portion of the middle lane has been widened (S-012).

[E-MODERATE] UKMTO recorded on September 11 that US-facilitated transits averaged 20 a day against six AIS-derived transits and that UAE-facilitated vessels transit with AIS off. Vortexa recorded 22 million Saudi barrels loaded across 12 vessels at Ras Tanura and Juaymah in the week of September 7 to 13 (S-011, S-010).

[E-MODERATE] Secretary Rubio telephoned Oman's foreign minister to acknowledge Oman's role in deescalation. Lavrov stated the Gulf-Iran meeting was postponed for several days and would convene in Oman with Iraq participating. Araghchi stated in Beijing that the June memorandum remains in force and that Iran has agreed with Oman on a plan to reopen the strait; Wang Yi urged both sides to return to the Islamabad memorandum. The Security Council votes on the 1737 Committee panel of experts on September 17 (S-029, S-027, S-028, S-037).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: The official figure of 40 ships a day is ten times the AIS-derived count and four times the Energy Secretary's Sunday figure of 10 million barrels a day; it is carried CONTESTED under source symmetry, as an allied official claim without independent corroboration beyond loadings data inside the Gulf. The judgment that Kpler-counted transits remain below 30 on a ten-day average through 301600Z (EF-005-P9) is assessed very likely with moderate confidence, on the assumption that US-escorted transits continue to run with AIS off and therefore outside the Kpler count. On the Council, three hypotheses were assessed: H1, Russia or China vetoes; H2, both abstain and the panel is renewed; H3, the vote is postponed. H1 is carried at roughly even chance with low confidence (EF-005-P2, KJ-2), on the assumption that Moscow and Beijing convert their stated position that the Council cannot reestablish the panel into a veto rather than an abstention; the March joint statement supports it, the eight rejected candidates support it, and the Xi visit in eight days and Bessent's weekend meeting cut against it. H2 is assessed unlikely; H3 is assessed unlikely given no reporting of a deferral. The evidence that would most change this is a published draft with a named sponsor. The judgment that no US-Iran agreement reopening Hormuz to unrestricted traffic is announced by 161600Z OCT (EF-005-P12, KJ-5) is assessed very likely with moderate confidence, on the assumption that neither side moves off stated conditions before the November 3 election.
