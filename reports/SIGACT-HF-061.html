7.3 The FAA Freezes Its Router Rollout as the Maker Blames the Setup

[E-MODERATE] The FAA said it addressed the initial cause by disabling the originating Juniper routers' function, disabled the same function on more than 60 others, and paused installations. An FAA spokesperson confirmed the pause. Hewlett Packard Enterprise, which owns Juniper, said the device worked as the installer programmed it and was not faulty. (S-009, S-010, S-011)

[E-LOW] Democratic senators on the Commerce Committee asked for a response by November 23; their letter says about 400 of more than 3,600 planned routers are installed and 69 are operational. The Transportation Secretary has said officials do not think a cyberattack caused the stop and that he will disclose it if one did. (S-012, S-013, S-014)

Attribution rung reached: 1 (activity observed); officials report no malicious actor.

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: The dispute is now between the router maker and the installer, which points to configuration rather than intrusion. A public statement of root cause by December 3 is a roughly even chance (HF-061-P2). The FAA resuming installations by December 3 is very unlikely (HF-061-P3). A lab finding of no malicious code by November 28 remains a roughly even chance (HF-060-P4). An FBI cyber case would change this.
