SECTION 1: BLUF

A federal threat bulletin issued in advance of the 25th anniversary of the September 11, 2001 attacks warns that foreign terrorist organizations "continue to encourage attacks in the United States and have released 9/11-related propaganda in advance of previous anniversaries of the 9/11 attacks," according to a bulletin reviewed by ABC News, and that the FBI is concerned that terrorism actors in the United States may be inspired to conduct violence or engage in hoax threats in response to the anniversary, with those actors having shown willingness to act with little to no warning [E-MODERATE / P-MODERATE]. The operative protective implication is the explicit pairing of anniversary symbolism with the Middle East conflict: the bulletin states that foreign terrorist organization messaging "in combination with ongoing conflicts in the Middle East could heighten existing grievances against the United States and contribute to mobilization to violence", which converts a commemorative-date concern into a war-duration concern that does not expire at midnight on September 11. Separately, the Iranian state-linked threat to the Homeland remains concentrated in the cyber-physical domain rather than the kinetic domain, with the State Department having posted a $10 million Rewards for Justice offer on September 3, 2026 for information leading to the identification or location of Amir Yaryab, head of the cyber operations division within the IRGC Cyber-Electronic Command [E-HIGH]. Security directors should treat the 72 hours following the anniversary, not the anniversary itself, as the period of greatest residual risk, because the hardening posture that protects commemorative events on September 11 typically relaxes on September 12 while the mobilization narrative persists [P-MODERATE]. No credible, specific, and imminent threat to a named U.S. location has been identified in available OSINT during the reporting period [E-MODERATE].

SECTION 3: STRATEGIC OVERVIEW

3.1 Anniversary Threat Environment and the Federal Bulletin

Federal authorities issued a threat bulletin ahead of the 25th anniversary. The FBI is concerned that terrorism actors in the United States may be inspired to conduct violence or engage in hoax threats in response to the 25th anniversary of the 9/11 attacks, and those actors have shown willingness to act with little to no warning [PRESS] [E-MODERATE]. Separately and on the record, a DHS spokesperson said on Thursday that the United States is facing "a heightened and increasingly complex terrorism threat environment," adding that the environment has "changed significantly since the attacks of September 11, 2001" [GOV -- ALLIED] [E-HIGH].

The department characterized the structural shift as follows: "Individuals and small cells now plot attacks independently, often motivated by a range of ideological and political goals," with foreign terrorist organizations such as ISIS and al-Qaeda continuing to seek ways to carry out or inspire attacks in the United States, while "domestic terrorists continue to plan acts of violence, often driven by anti-government views and a desire to destabilize society" [GOV -- ALLIED] [E-HIGH]. Officials state that in the past year and a half the FBI has arrested at least 16 people inside the U.S. homeland for allegedly plotting or supporting plots to kill Americans on behalf of al-Qaeda or ISIS [GOV -- ALLIED] [E-MODERATE]. This figure is attributed to unnamed officials rather than to a published enforcement dataset, and it is not independently verifiable in available OSINT. It is carried as an indicative rather than an audited number.

Analytical treatment of allied official statements. Applying the same standard used for adversary claims, two observations are documented. First, the "at least 16" figure is a floor, is officially sourced, and is unfalsifiable from open sources; it should not be used as a trend measurement against prior years. Second, the department's framing emphasizes the dispersion of the threat, which is analytically sound but also has the secondary effect of describing a threat picture against which no specific preventive failure can be attributed. This is noted for calibration, not as an allegation of bad faith. Both observations reduce the evidentiary weight assigned to official characterizations of aggregate threat volume; neither reduces the weight assigned to the specific warning content of the bulletin.

3.2 Emerging Capability Concerns Flagged by DHS

The department raised two capability vectors in the same engagement. On artificial intelligence, the spokesperson stated that "terrorists are increasingly experimenting with generative AI, not only to create and circulate violent online propaganda, but to explore ways to use explosives and biological agents to conduct attacks," and that AI advancements "could make terrorist activity faster, more scalable, and harder to detect" [GOV -- ALLIED] [E-HIGH / P-LOW]. Evidentiary confidence is high that the statement was made. Predictive confidence that generative AI materially changes attack capability inside the assessment windows of this product is low: no publicly documented U.S. plot in the reporting period has been attributed to AI-derived explosive or biological capability, and the statement is a capability-trend warning rather than an incident report. Security directors should not reallocate resources on the basis of this item alone.

On uncrewed systems, law enforcement is also concerned about the use of drones, and the department takes seriously the possibility that a drone could be used despite the United States not having experienced a large-scale terrorist attack involving drones [GOV -- ALLIED] [E-MODERATE]. This is the more operationally actionable of the two capability flags and is reflected in Section 6.

3.3 Congressional Assessment of the Domestic Threat Landscape

The oversight committee published a structural assessment in the same window. The House Committee on Homeland Security called for fresh security reforms in a report released Thursday ahead of the anniversary, titled "25th Anniversary Report: National Security Improvements and the Domestic Threat Landscape 25 Years After 9/11/01," which assesses progress in strengthening U.S. homeland security since the attacks and outlines more than 20 recommendations covering intelligence-sharing, terrorism prevention, artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, border security and emergency preparedness [GOV -- ALLIED] [E-HIGH]. The committee said the recommendations followed hearings, briefings, roundtable discussions and site visits conducted over the past year to assess transportation security, counterterrorism, information-sharing, federal law enforcement coordination, critical infrastructure protection and the operations of agencies under the department [GOV -- ALLIED] [E-HIGH].

Assessment. A congressional finding that deficiencies remain 25 years after the attacks is a statement about institutional capacity, not about imminent threat. It is analytically relevant to this product for one reason only: it is a public, official acknowledgment that identified gaps exist in the federal layers on which private-sector security programs rely. Protective planning should assume that federal advisory and information-sharing support is a contributing input, not a sufficient one [P-MODERATE].

3.4 Iranian State-Sponsored Activity Targeting the Homeland

Iranian-attributed homeland activity in the current war continues to concentrate in cyber-physical targeting of operational technology rather than in kinetic plotting. The authoring agencies published an advisory on April 2026, titled Iranian-Affiliated Cyber Actors Exploit Programmable Logic Controllers (PLC) Across US Critical Infrastructure, which CISA, the FBI, the EPA and other U.S. government partners updated on July 22, 2026 [GOV -- ALLIED] [E-HIGH]. The advisory reports that the FBI observed Iranian-affiliated APT actors targeting internet-exposed PLCs with the intent to cause disruptions, including maliciously interacting with project files and manipulating data displayed on HMI and SCADA displays, and that Iranian-affiliated targeting campaigns against U.S. critical infrastructure have recently escalated, likely in response to hostilities between Iran, and the United States and Israel [GOV -- ALLIED] [E-HIGH].

The July update added a technically significant detail. At one U.S. victim, the FBI observed the actors download a malicious project file to a targeted PLC using configuration software; analysis indicated the project file retained ladder logic for downstream function but added logic that overrode specific instruction sets responsible for maintaining safe operating parameters [GOV -- ALLIED] [E-HIGH]. This is the single most consequential technical fact in the current Homeland picture and is developed in Section 5.

Observed consequences have been reported across sectors: organizations from multiple U.S. critical infrastructure sectors have reported disruptions including configuration wiping, software-based mechanical sensor tampering, and disruption of human machine interfaces, resulting in operational disruption and financial loss [GOV -- ALLIED] [E-HIGH]. The equipment focus has been specified: the hackers have targeted programmable logic controllers made by Rockwell Automation/Allen-Bradley in attacks involving malicious interactions with project files, leading to data manipulation on both the human machine interface and supervisory control and data acquisition displays [TRADE] [E-HIGH].

Targeting has reportedly broadened in the weeks preceding this report. Iranian government-linked hackers have widened their targeting of American critical infrastructure, attempting to breach systems tied to water utilities, telecommunications networks and energy providers in the weeks leading up to September 2026, with NBC News reporting on September 2, 2026, citing four people with access to government and industry cyber-threat information, that the attempts have so far focused on internet-exposed automated control systems and have not caused major damage [PRESS] [E-MODERATE]. The addition of telecommunications to the confirmed target set is a change from the April advisory sectors and is carried forward as an established baseline.

Documented conflict between official and press accounts. Available reporting records a material discrepancy in the scale of the July water sector incidents: a New York Times account of U.S. intelligence blaming Iran-linked hackers for July attacks on more than 100 water systems stands against the CISA count of 12 states dated August 5 [PRESS / GOV -- ALLIED] [E-LOW] (Source 12). These two figures measure different units (systems versus states) and are not strictly contradictory, but they have been used interchangeably in secondary coverage. Regent Intel Group does not treat either figure as established. The discrepancy is unresolved and is listed in Information Gaps.

3.5 Iranian Network Targeting: Leadership Exposure

The State Department publicly named the IRGC cyber operations leadership. Rewards for Justice is seeking information on Amir Yaryab, a senior official in Iran's IRGC Cyber-Electronic Command who leads the command's Cyber Operations Command and directs multiple IRGC-CEC components including Shahid Hemmat and Shahid Shushtari, groups that have conducted cyber and cyber-enabled information operations targeting critical infrastructure sectors including defense, news, shipping, travel including hotels and airlines, and energy [GOV -- ALLIED] [E-HIGH]. The offer of up to $10 million applies to information leading to the identification or location of any person who, while acting at the direction or under the control of a foreign government, participates in malicious cyber activities against U.S. critical infrastructure in violation of the Computer Fraud and Abuse Act [GOV -- ALLIED] [E-HIGH]. Yaryab allegedly oversees cyber operations conducted by IRGC-CEC-affiliated groups including CyberAv3ngers, Dadeh Afzar Arman and Mehrsam Andisheh Saz Nik [PRESS] [E-HIGH].

The named subordinate group has established U.S. sector history: in late 2023, Iranian IRGC-affiliated actors using the CyberAv3ngers persona targeted internet-exposed Unitronics Vision Series programmable logic controllers and human-machine interfaces, including devices used by U.S. water and wastewater facilities [PRESS] [E-HIGH]. The travel, hotel, and airline sectors named in the reward listing are a notable expansion of the publicly acknowledged Iranian target set beyond water and energy and carry direct implications for corporate security programs in hospitality and aviation support services.

3.6 Iranian-Directed Kinetic Plotting Against U.S. Targets

The most developed publicly documented Iranian-directed kinetic plotting against U.S. domestic targets in this conflict remains the Kata'ib Hizballah case. The Justice Department announced the arrest of Mohammad Baqer Saad Dawood Al-Saadi, an Iraqi national and senior member of Kata'ib Hizballah, a U.S. designated foreign terrorist organization, charged by complaint with six counts of terrorism-related offenses for his activities as an operative of Kata'ib Hizballah and Iran's IRGC, including involvement in nearly 20 attacks and attempted attacks throughout Europe and the United States [GOV -- ALLIED] [E-HIGH]. In recent months Al-Saadi allegedly directed and urged others to attack U.S. and Israeli interests, including by killing Americans and Jews, to further the terrorist goals of Kata'ib Hizballah and the IRGC [GOV -- ALLIED] [E-HIGH].

The domestic targeting detail is the operative element for protective planning. In the United States, Al-Saadi allegedly directed an undercover law enforcement officer he believed capable of carrying out attacks, instructing the officer to target a prominent synagogue in Manhattan, a Jewish institution in Los Angeles, and a Jewish institution in Scottsdale, Arizona; he texted the officer photographs and maps of each location and discussed whether to use an improvised explosive device or set the New York synagogue ablaze, and no attacks were carried out on U.S. soil [PRESS] [E-MODERATE]. Prosecutors allege that following the start of the military conflict in late February 2026, Al-Saadi became a central figure in coordinating international retaliation through a front group to activate cells, frequently using teenage suspects, across Europe and Canada [PRESS] [E-MODERATE]. These are allegations in a pending prosecution and are labeled as such.

Assessment. The Al-Saadi model is the most analytically useful template available for how Iranian-aligned networks approach U.S. domestic targeting in this war: remote direction from outside U.S. territory, recruitment of low-sophistication or young proxies with no prior organizational record, target selection favoring Jewish community institutions, and tactical flexibility between arson and improvised explosives [E-MODERATE / P-MODERATE]. Arson is specifically noted because it requires no procurement signature and defeats most of the detection architecture built around explosive precursors.

3.7 ISIS and al-Qaeda Inspired Threat

The inspired threat stream remains the highest-volume disruption category. The NYPD intelligence assessment characterizes the change: "The threat is quite different from where it was 25 years ago. It's decentralized geographically with the rise of Al-Qaeda and ISIS affiliates globally," NYPD Deputy Commissioner of Intelligence and Counterterrorism Rebecca Weiner said [GOV -- ALLIED] [E-HIGH]. In March, the U.S. intelligence community released a report on the state of worldwide threats and noted that while ISIS and al-Qaeda may be largely relying on social media to reach America, their numbers are actually growing overseas [GOV -- ALLIED] [E-MODERATE].

Recent-period enforcement establishes the tempo baseline carried forward in this series. Over the course of 2025 the FBI recorded 707 counter-terrorism arrests and 640 successful disruptions of planned attacks [PRESS, citing official statement] [E-LOW]. This figure derives from a director's social media statement reported by a secondary outlet and is not drawn from an audited enforcement dataset. It is included for tempo context only and should not be cited as a verified statistic.

The December 2025 cluster remains the most recent detailed disruption set in available OSINT: arrests of four members of the Turtle Island Liberation Front on December 12 over an alleged New Year's Eve bombing campaign targeting businesses in Southern California; a December 21 Pennsylvania arrest of an individual reportedly researching ISIS propaganda who officials said intended to launch an explosive attack campaign; a December 29 Texas arrest of an ISIS sympathizer for allegedly attempting to provide bomb-making materials to a foreign terrorist organization; and a New Year's Eve multi-agency arrest in New York and North Carolina of a man claiming to be an "ISIS soldier" who authorities say planned a mass casualty attack [PRESS] [E-MODERATE]. The ideological spread across that single month, spanning far-left anti-capitalist and ISIS-inspired actors, is the clearest available illustration of the dispersion the department describes.

3.8 Domestic Violent Extremism, All Ideologies

Available analytic reporting indicates a methodological shift rather than a volume shift. Recent high-profile assassinations and assassination attempts in the United States have very likely increased the probability of domestic violent extremists considering limited-scope methods, with violent extremists having glorified alleged perpetrators and promoted narratives suggesting that targeted attacks against high-profile public figures more effectively achieve political and ideological goals than mass-casualty attacks [RESEARCH] [E-MODERATE]. Forward-looking analytic work identifies additional vectors: an increasing normalization of political violence and a growing trend of non-ideological violent acts and composite violent extremism, alongside the rise of memetic and nihilist violent extremism leading to offline violence, often perpetrated by minors [RESEARCH] [E-MODERATE / P-MODERATE].

For the anniversary window specifically, specialist assessment holds that the 25th anniversary may hold symbolic significance for extremist propaganda and self-radicalized individuals, increasing the likelihood of opportunistic or commemorative attacks rather than coordinated mass-casualty plots, with such incidents more likely to target high-visibility public spaces, transportation systems, and religious or symbolic sites, which combine accessibility with psychological impact [RESEARCH] [E-MODERATE / P-MODERATE]. Regent Intel Group concurs with this characterization and adopts it as the working baseline for the anniversary period.

3.9 Transnational Criminal Organization Nexus

Open-source analytic reporting flags a policy-driven escalation pathway: cartels and gangs targeted following Foreign Terrorist Organization classification could seek to engage in horizontal escalation and commit attacks in the United States, with cartel leaders potentially responding with attacks on American law enforcement officers and infrastructure or, more feasibly, attacks on American civilians in shopping malls, on buses, at concerts, and in other crowded places [RESEARCH] [E-MODERATE / P-LOW]. Predictive confidence is low. This is a reasoned forecast from a credible institution, not an indicator-driven assessment, and no observed TCO attack against U.S. civilian soft targets has been identified in available OSINT during this reporting period. It is carried as an analytical flag requiring independent verification before any protective resource shift.

A related convergence indicator is the reported federal enforcement posture: the FBI is turning to artificial intelligence as a tool to identify threats and fight crime, targeting transnational criminal organizations and drug cartels, Deputy Director Christopher Raia told CBS News [PRESS] [E-MODERATE]. This indicates continued federal prioritization of the TCO stream but says nothing about TCO intent against soft targets.
