7.2 Russian State-Directed Repression Reaches the Homeland

[E-MODERATE] The indictment describes a group prosecutors call the RIS Network: Yuri Khrameev, 63, described as a former Russian intelligence colonel; his son Kirill, 27; Cuban nationals Oemis Romagoza Durruthy and Yaidel Delgado Suarez, both 35; and Venezuelan national Angel Eduardo Castro, 22. All five are charged with terrorism finance conspiracy; Yuri Khrameev, Suarez, and Castro are also charged with murder-for-hire conspiracy. All are believed to be in Russia and at large (S-001, S-002, S-004).

[E-MODERATE] In July and August 2026, Castro and one of the Cuban defendants allegedly recruited a Brooklyn resident, paid $1,000 to $1,500, to photograph two locations associated with an unnamed Russian dissident whom investigators believed was in the Washington area. The Center Square reported that the surveillant was then offered $40,000 to eliminate the victim and declined, that a Lithuania target was priced at $25,000, and that Durruthy arranged logistics for attacks in Prague and Lithuania. The recruits were not charged (S-001, S-005, S-003).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: Evidence chain. OBSERVED: a Brooklyn resident was paid to surveil two locations tied to a dissident in July to August 2026 (S-001). KNOWN: the indictment charges that the taskers worked for Russian intelligence services and that the same network arranged attacks in Lithuania and Czechia (S-002, S-005). HISTORICAL: Russian services have contracted third-country nationals for sabotage and killings in Europe since 2022, per the European cases the indictment references. PATTERN: this is the first charged Russian-directed killing plot with a US target since the 2020s cases, so the Homeland count is one, an observation and not a pattern. INFERENCE: the network treated the United States as an operating area on the same terms as Europe. HIGHER-ORDER INFERENCE: Russian services judge the cost of a Homeland killing operation acceptable during the Iran war. Attribution rung reached: 6 (state direction) as charged in the indictment; independent evidence in OSINT reaches rung 4 (organizational association) through the defendants' described roles, and the Kremlin has not denied the allegations. Three hypotheses were assessed: H1, the plot was tasked by a Russian intelligence service as charged; H2, a freelance criminal network sold the plot to Russian buyers; H3, the case is entrapment-adjacent with no live tasking. H1 is least contradicted and is carried as the working characterization (E-MODERATE), since the indictment names a former intelligence colonel and links the same logistics to European attacks. H2 is assessed unlikely; H3 is assessed very unlikely given the recruit's account of a $40,000 offer he declined. The single piece of evidence that would most change this is the recruit's testimony or the identity of the tasking service. The judgment that DOJ charges at least one additional US-located person in connection with the network by 161600Z OCT (HF-005-P9) is assessed unlikely with low confidence, on the assumption that the recruited surveillants cooperated and were treated as witnesses rather than defendants.
