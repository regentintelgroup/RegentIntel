[E-HIGH] The Justice Department stated that the indictment charges five members of a network working for Russian intelligence services with conspiring to finance terrorism, and three of them with murder-for-hire conspiracy, and that the network recruited US persons to conduct surveillance in the United States (S-001, S-002).

[E-HIGH] The department named Yuri Khrameev, 63, a former colonel in the Russian intelligence services; his son Kirill Khrameev, 27, an officer of the Federal Security Service; Cuban nationals Oemis Romagoza Durruthy and Yaidel Delgado Suarez, both 35; and Venezuelan national Angel Eduardo Castro, 22, and stated the network has paid individuals in the United States and elsewhere since 2024 (S-001, S-004, S-045).

[E-MODERATE] In July and August 2026, Castro and one of the Cuban defendants allegedly recruited a Venezuelan citizen living in Brooklyn, paid $1,000 to $1,500, to photograph two locations associated with an unnamed Russian dissident whom investigators believed was in the Washington area. The Center Square reported that the surveillant was then offered $40,000 to eliminate the victim and declined, that a Lithuania target was priced at $25,000, and that Durruthy arranged logistics for attacks in Prague and Lithuania. (S-003, S-007, S-005).

[E-MODERATE] In the Lithuania plot a US citizen was paid $200 to photograph the target's address in 2025 and then offered about $25,000 to kill him. Neither recruit was charged; all five defendants are believed to be in Russia (S-046, S-006).

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: The evidence chain runs: OBSERVED, a Brooklyn resident was paid to surveil two locations tied to a dissident in July to August 2026 (S-003); KNOWN, the indictment charges that the taskers worked for Russian intelligence services and that the same network arranged attacks in Lithuania and Czechia (S-001, S-007); HISTORICAL, Russian services have contracted third-country nationals for sabotage and killings in Europe since 2022, per the European cases the indictment references; PATTERN, this is the first charged Russian-directed killing plot with a US target in this series, so the Homeland count is one, an observation and not a pattern; INFERENCE, the network treated the United States as an operating area on the same terms as Europe; HIGHER-ORDER INFERENCE, Russian services judged the cost of a Homeland killing operation acceptable during the Iran war.
ANALYST ASSESSMENT: Attribution rung reached: 6 (state direction) as charged in the indictment; independent evidence in OSINT reaches rung 4 (organizational association) through the defendants' described roles, and the Kremlin has not denied the allegations. Three hypotheses were assessed: H1, the plot was tasked by a Russian intelligence service as charged; H2, a freelance criminal network sold the plot to Russian buyers; H3, no live tasking existed and the case rests on the recruits' accounts. H1 is least contradicted by available evidence and is carried as the working characterization (E-MODERATE), since the indictment names a former intelligence colonel and a serving FSB officer and links the same logistics to European attacks. H2 is assessed unlikely. H3 is assessed very unlikely given the recruit's account of a $40,000 offer he declined. The single piece of evidence that would most change this assessment is the identity of the tasking service or the recruit's testimony.
