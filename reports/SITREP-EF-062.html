7.1 Iran Fires on Kuwait for the First Time Since September

[E-MODERATE] Kuwait activated its air defenses against drones on November 18, the first such attack since early September. Iran's army said its drones targeted Patriot systems and launch platforms at Ali Al Salem Air Base. Kuwait and Bahrain both said they intercepted the attacks, and neither reported damage. The U.S. military said no U.S. personnel were injured. (S-001, S-002, S-003, S-004)

[E-LOW] Bahrain's civil defense evacuated a multistory building in Souq Waqif as a precaution and put out a fire inside; the cause is under investigation. Kuwait's Foreign Ministry called the attack a flagrant violation of its sovereignty. (S-006, S-008)

Attribution rung reached: 6 (state direction) for the November 18 attacks, which Iran's army claims as its own operation.

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: Iran answered the Qeshm strike by reaching past Bahrain to Kuwait, the second host state in five days, while keeping each salvo small enough to be intercepted. The pattern holds: each U.S. action draws Iranian fire at host bases within about 48 hours. A further Iranian launch at a U.S.-hosting base by November 21 remains very likely (EF-061-P1). Confirmed damage at a base would change this.

7.2 Interception Debris Damages Riyadh's Tallest Tower

[E-MODERATE] Coalition spokesman Turki al-Malki said on November 19 that the coalition addressed a missile threat aimed at Riyadh and that the initial assessment showed debris impact. Witnesses told AFP the damaged building was the Public Investment Fund Tower, the 385-meter centerpiece of the King Abdullah Financial District. Civil defense issued a danger warning and later lifted it. (S-014, S-015)

[E-LOW] The coalition also said it intercepted a Houthi cruise missile aimed at southern Saudi Arabia. On November 18 it said it destroyed 60 Houthi targets in Sanaa, Amran, Saada, and Hodeidah, including tunnels storing ballistic missiles. The Houthis have gained ground inside Yemen. (S-016, S-017, S-018)

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: The financial district has now been hit twice in eleven days, both times by debris rather than a direct strike, which means interception over the city does not protect its towers. Coalition strikes on missile storage have not slowed launches. Reported damage or casualties in Saudi Arabia from a Houthi launch by 1800Z on November 22 is a roughly even chance (EF-062-P2). A week without a launch toward Riyadh would change this.
