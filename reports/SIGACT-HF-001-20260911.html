SECTION 1: BLUF

Federal authorities issued a joint threat bulletin on September 10, 2026, warning that foreign terrorist organizations "continue to encourage attacks in the United States and have released 9/11-related propaganda in advance of previous anniversaries of the 9/11 attacks," and that terrorism actors in the U.S. may be inspired to conduct violence or engage in hoax threats in response to the 25th anniversary, with those actors having shown willingness to act with little to no warning [GOV / E-HIGH]. The bulletin lands on the same day the Department of State announced Operation Economic Outcast against Kata'ib Hizballah, Hizballah, and the financial networks sustaining Iran's regional destabilization campaign, explicitly citing KH's recent plot to attack Jewish institutions in the United States [GOV / E-HIGH]. Regent Intel Group assesses that the dominant near-term Homeland risk is not a directed mass-casualty operation but a low-signature, low-warning actor acting on symbolic date resonance, with the Iranian state-sponsored vector operating in parallel through cyber effects against operational technology and through proxy-contracted lethal plotting [E-HIGH / P-MODERATE]. For protective posture, this means anniversary-period surge measures should be weighted toward Jewish community facilities, houses of worship, transit nodes, and memorial or commemorative gatherings, not toward hardened aviation targets, and should extend past the calendar date rather than standing down at midnight. Security directors should treat the absence of a publicly identified specific credible threat as an intelligence gap rather than as an all-clear.

SECTION 3: STRATEGIC OVERVIEW

3.1 Active Federal Advisory Posture

The most consequential development of the reporting period is advisory, not kinetic. A federal bulletin reviewed by ABC News on September 10, 2026, warned that foreign terrorist organizations continue to encourage attacks in the United States and have historically released 9/11-related propaganda ahead of anniversaries; that the FBI is concerned U.S.-based terrorism actors may be inspired to conduct violence or hoax threats around the 25th anniversary; and that FTO messaging combined with ongoing Middle East conflicts could heighten existing grievances against the United States and contribute to mobilization to violence [GOV via PRESS / E-HIGH].

Separately, a DHS spokesperson stated on September 10, 2026, that the United States faces "a heightened and increasingly complex terrorism threat environment" that has changed significantly since 2001, noting that while centralized international organizations remain a threat, the landscape is now broader and more dispersed, with individuals and small cells plotting independently [GOV -- ALLIED / E-HIGH]. The spokesperson further stated that ISIS and al-Qaeda continue to seek ways to carry out or inspire attacks in the U.S., and that domestic terrorists continue to plan acts of violence often driven by anti-government views [GOV -- ALLIED / E-HIGH].

Analytical note on source symmetry and format. Regent Intel Group applies the same rigor to this allied government characterization as to adversary claims. Two observations follow. First, the federal warning was delivered as a law enforcement bulletin and a spokesperson background statement, not as a Secretary-level National Terrorism Advisory System bulletin with a published expiration date. Second, no specific credible threat to a named location has been publicly identified in available OSINT. The assessed implication is that the messaging is calibrated to the calendar and to the general conflict environment rather than to a discrete piece of targeting intelligence [E-MODERATE / P-MODERATE]. This assessment would change if an NTAS bulletin is issued or if a specific threat is publicly attributed.

3.2 Iranian State-Sponsored Activity Targeting the Homeland

The Iranian lethal-plotting vector is the best-evidenced state-sponsored threat to the Homeland in this baseline. The Department of Justice announced in May 2026 the arrest of Mohammad Baqer Saad Dawood Al-Saadi, an Iraqi national and senior member of Kata'ib Hizballah, charged by complaint with six terrorism-related offenses for activities as an operative of KH and the IRGC, including involvement in nearly 20 attacks and attempted attacks throughout Europe and the United States [GOV / E-HIGH]. DOJ alleged that in approximately April and May 2026 Al-Saadi attempted to coordinate attacks to kill individuals in the United States, and that on or about April 3, 2026, he sent an undercover law enforcement officer photographs and maps showing the exact location of a prominent New York synagogue plus Jewish institutions in Los Angeles and Scottsdale, directing attacks against those targets [GOV / E-HIGH]. Methods discussed allegedly included arson and improvised explosive devices [GOV via PRESS / E-HIGH].

The State Department's September 10, 2026 action confirms that this network remains a live policy concern rather than a closed case. Operation Economic Outcast targets KH, Hizballah, and broader financial networks, and the announcement states that KH has intensified its threat to U.S. interests including a recent plot to attack Jewish institutions in the United States [GOV / E-HIGH]. Assessment: the sanctions action is a financial-disruption measure taken after the operational disruption, and does not by itself indicate new plotting [E-HIGH / P-MODERATE].

3.3 Critical Infrastructure and Cyber-Physical Convergence

This is the vector where adversary capability and U.S. exposure are most demonstrably converging. CISA and partner agencies published advisory AA26-097A on April 7, 2026, providing tactics, techniques, procedures, and indicators of compromise related to ongoing cyber exploitation of internet-connected operational technology devices by Iranian-affiliated advanced persistent threat actors [GOV / E-HIGH]. The advisory was updated on July 22, 2026, adding guidance on detecting malicious changes in reusable code modules within Rockwell Automation PLC programs and expanding the manufacturer scope to include observed targeting of Schneider Electric, Siemens, and potentially other branded PLCs [GOV / E-HIGH]. Targeted sectors include water and wastewater systems, energy, government facilities, and local municipalities, and confirmed victims have experienced operational disruption and financial losses [GOV via TRADE / E-HIGH].

Activity has continued and broadened through the reporting year. NBC News reported on September 2, 2026, citing four people with access to government and industry cyberthreat information, that Iranian hackers have targeted not only U.S. water systems but also telecommunications, energy, and other infrastructure in recent weeks [PRESS / E-MODERATE]. The attempted attacks were characterized as unsuccessful but as underscoring Iran's readiness to retaliate against the United States beyond its interests in the Middle East [PRESS / E-MODERATE]. This activity follows a July wave in which more than 100 U.S. water and wastewater systems across 12 states were targeted [PRESS, secondary aggregation / E-MODERATE]. The FBI stated in the preceding month that it was investigating cyberattacks on municipal water systems in at least seven states [GOV via PRESS / E-MODERATE].

[ADVERSARY-SOURCED] A Telegram channel presenting itself as a voice for Iranian cyber operations, styled APT IRAN, threatened attacks on U.S. infrastructure, stating that the United States would soon witness unexpected and critical events in the energy, water, and telecommunications industries. The channel provided no evidence of successful cyber operations against the United States. Regent Intel Group treats this as declaratory signaling, not as evidence of capability. Weight: low. See Section 5.3.

Structural caveat, documented for objectivity. CSIS assessed in May 2026 that the United States has experienced gaps in leadership and cuts to key agencies including CISA and ODNI, funding losses for information-sharing centers and state and local authorities, and that during a Department of Homeland Security shutdown the Acting Director of CISA stated CISA could not perform outreach and preparatory activity necessary to counter cyber threats [RESEARCH / E-MODERATE]. This is a defender-side degradation indicator and is recorded here because ICD 203 objectivity requires documenting evidence unfavorable to the allied posture with the same rigor applied to adversary claims.

3.4 ISIS and AQ-Inspired Threats and Disrupted Plots

No U.S.-soil jihadist-inspired attack or disruption was publicly reported within the 24 to 48 hour window. Recent reporting establishes an active transnational disruption tempo with a U.S. nexus. FDD's Long War Journal reported on September 8, 2026, that French authorities in early September detained three men suspected of criminal association with a terrorist organization for the purpose of preparing acts of terrorism, following a July investigation into a WhatsApp group, with the suspects allegedly considering attacks against France's Jewish community while preparing to join the Islamic State in Africa, and that one suspect had been in contact with a 35-year-old woman from Albany, New York, whom the FBI detained in mid-August for allegedly attempting to provide material support to the Islamic State [RESEARCH / E-MODERATE].

Assessment: the Albany linkage is analytically significant out of proportion to its scale. It indicates that U.S.-based ISIS-sympathizer radicalization is networked into European cells through commodity encrypted messaging, which shortens the path from consumption of propaganda to contact with externally directed facilitation [E-MODERATE / P-MODERATE].

3.5 Domestic Violent Extremism, All Ideologies

No mass-casualty DVE attack was publicly reported in the reporting window. The established structural position is that lone actors and small cells remain the dominant lethal modality across the ideological spectrum. Recorded Future assessed that recent high-profile assassinations and assassination attempts in the U.S. have very likely increased the probability of DVEs considering limited-scope methods, and that violent extremists have glorified alleged perpetrators and promoted narratives that targeted attacks against high-profile public figures more effectively achieve political goals than mass-casualty attacks [RESEARCH / E-MODERATE]. The Soufan Center assessed a domestic landscape characterized by increasing normalization of political violence and growth in non-ideological and composite violent extremism, alongside rising memetic and nihilist violent extremism producing offline violence, often perpetrated by minors [RESEARCH / E-MODERATE].

Enforcement-side baseline: FBI figures for 2025 reported 707 counter-terrorism arrests and 640 disruptions of planned attacks [GOV via PRESS, secondary / E-LOW]. Regent Intel Group flags that these aggregate figures come from a political communications channel rather than a primary statistical publication and have not been corroborated against a primary FBI data release in available OSINT. They are reported for context and are not used as the basis for any assessment in this product.

3.6 Aviation and Transportation Security

TSA commemorated the 25th anniversary on September 11, 2026, reaffirming its commitment to protecting transportation systems and staying ahead of evolving threats [GOV / E-HIGH]. TSA noted responsibility beyond airports for ports, railways, and pipelines, as well as major events including America's 250th anniversary celebrations and the FIFA World Cup 2026 [GOV / E-HIGH]. The agency stated it will continue adapting to emerging threats including cyber risks and unmanned aircraft systems [GOV / E-HIGH].

Assessment: hardened commercial aviation remains a low-probability, high-consequence target set. The displacement logic that has governed Western attack patterns for a decade points threat actors toward surface transportation, landside airport areas, and open-access gathering points rather than through the screening checkpoint [E-MODERATE / P-MODERATE]. Independent analysis assesses that anniversary-linked incidents are more likely to target high-visibility public spaces, transportation systems, and religious or symbolic sites, which combine accessibility with psychological impact [RESEARCH / E-MODERATE].

3.7 Unmanned Aircraft Systems

UAS is the fastest-moving gap in the Homeland protective architecture. CFR assessed on September 8, 2026, that the most pressing threat posed by terrorists and hostile actors today comes from unmanned aerial systems, and that technology is driving new terrorism threats to the U.S. homeland [RESEARCH / E-MODERATE]. CFR further assessed that the United States marks the anniversary with a stunning lack of preparedness to handle mounting new threats and resurgent old ones [RESEARCH / E-MODERATE].

The scale of the problem is now quantified. As of June 20, 2026, DHS and the FBI had recorded more than 600 drone incursions into restricted airspace across U.S. World Cup host-city venues [GOV via TRADE / E-HIGH]. During the 2026 FIFA World Cup, U.S. agencies seized more than 700 drones in the vicinity of match sites, including 10 unauthorized aircraft intercepted by F-16 fighter jets during the final in New Jersey [TRADE / E-MODERATE]. Authority has expanded in response: the SAFER SKIES Act, enacted within the FY2026 NDAA, authorizes state, local, tribal, and territorial agencies to detect and mitigate credible UAS threats to people, venues used for large-scale public gatherings, critical infrastructure, and correctional facilities, subject to conditions [GOV / E-HIGH]. The implementing rule acknowledged that federal counter-UAS resources are not sufficient for the scope of the threat [GOV / E-HIGH].

Assessment: the 600-plus incursion figure is overwhelmingly attributable to careless and clueless operators rather than hostile intent. Its analytical value is as a signal-to-noise measurement. A saturated detection environment is precisely the condition under which a single hostile airframe is most likely to be misclassified as routine [E-HIGH / P-MODERATE]. Open-air fan zones and similar publicly accessible gathering spaces often lack systematic counter-UAS coverage, and perimeter fences, magnetometers, bag checks, and credential verification are rendered irrelevant by an airborne threat [RESEARCH / E-MODERATE].

3.8 TCO Nexus With Terrorism

The Department of State designated Tren de Aragua, MS-13, Cartel de Sinaloa, CJNG, Cartel del Noreste, La Nueva Familia Michoacana, Cartel de Golfo, and Carteles Unidos as Foreign Terrorist Organizations and Specially Designated Global Terrorists [GOV / E-HIGH]. DOJ stated that since January 20, 2025, it had federally charged more than 260 members and associates of Tren de Aragua, and the pace of designations has increased, with five additional Latin American criminal groups designated since the end of May 2026 [GOV via TRADE / E-MODERATE].

The analytically important development is not the designation architecture but the observed convergence. In the KH case, the complaint alleges Al-Saadi provided targeting photographs and maps to an undercover officer who was posing as a Mexican cartel member [GOV via PRESS / E-HIGH]. Assessment: IRGC-linked external operations planning has demonstrably incorporated a willingness to contract lethal tasking to criminal intermediaries believed to be cartel-affiliated. This is the operative TCO-terrorism nexus for Homeland protective planning, and it is more consequential than the separate question of whether designated cartels will themselves conduct ideological attacks [E-HIGH / P-MODERATE].
