Section 3: Strategic Overview

[E-MODERATE / P-MODERATE] The Homeland threat from Iranian state and proxy actors is assessed as ELEVATED and trending upward. Three converging factors drive this assessment. First, the dual-chokepoint escalation of September 10-11 demonstrates Iran's willingness and capability to execute coordinated multi-theater operations through its proxy network; this same network architecture includes nodes with Western Hemisphere reach (IRGC Quds Force external operations, Hezbollah international networks). Second, the approaching October 7 anniversary creates an annual threat spike for attacks against Israeli, Jewish, and U.S.-associated targets globally; Israeli security services assessed in the reporting period that this plotting is intensifying. Third, the IRGC's cyber campaign against U.S. critical infrastructure has matured from opportunistic defacement to systematic exploitation of industrial control systems, with the CyberAv3ngers group now capable of altering physical process control logic while feeding operators false readings.

Three hypotheses were assessed for the Homeland threat trajectory. H1: Iran maintains cyber and influence operations but does not activate kinetic Homeland operations, reserving that capability as a deterrent. H2: Iran activates pre-positioned assets for a limited retaliatory operation (assassination targeting, infrastructure sabotage) in response to a specific U.S. escalation. H3: Iran or its proxies execute an unsophisticated lone-wolf-style attack exploiting the threat environment. H1 is least contradicted by available evidence; Iran has not conducted a successful kinetic operation on U.S. soil since the conflict began despite demonstrated intent (the FBI's February 2026 California drone warning). H2 cannot be excluded and would be triggered by a specific escalation event (e.g., strike on Iranian nuclear facilities or Fordow). H3 remains an ongoing background risk elevated by the conflict environment but is not linked to specific indicators in the current reporting period. Section 5: Analytical Development

5.1 CyberAv3ngers: From Disruption to Destruction

The IRGC Cyber-Electronic Command's CyberAv3ngers group represents the most mature and persistent Iranian cyber threat to U.S. critical infrastructure. The threat has evolved through three distinct phases since the conflict began:

Phase 1 (Pre-war through March 2026): Opportunistic exploitation of Israeli-manufactured Unitronics PLCs, primarily in water and wastewater systems. At least 75 devices compromised. Custom ladder logic deployed to replace valid control code. The Municipal Water Authority of Aliquippa, Pennsylvania, and Irish water systems were among confirmed victims.

Phase 2 (April 2026): Following the CISA/FBI/NSA/EPA/DOE/USCYBERCOM joint advisory AA26-097A (April 7, 2026), the group shifted targeting to Rockwell Automation Logix controllers, exploiting CVE-2021-22681, a CVSS 9.8 authentication bypass. Censys identified 5,219 Rockwell Automation devices globally responding to EtherNet/IP protocols, with 74.6 percent located in the United States. Sectors targeted expanded to include energy and government services.

Phase 3 (July-September 2026): CISA updated advisory AA26-097A on July 22, 2026, adding new indicators of compromise. The August 2026 FBI indictment of 17 Iranians charged with conducting massive cyber theft on behalf of the IRGC confirmed the scale of Iranian cyber operations. NBC reported in September 2026 that targeting had expanded to telecommunications networks. The group now demonstrates the ability to alter physical process control logic while maintaining false readings on operator SCADA displays, a capability that transitions the threat from disruption to potential destruction. [OSINT-GOV: CISA AA26-097A, FBI indictment Aug 2026; OSINT-PRESS: NBC Sep 2026; OSINT-RESEARCH: Tenable, CSIS, FDD; OSINT-TRADE: Censys]

ANALYST ASSESSMENT: The CyberAv3ngers campaign is assessed as a deliberate capability-building program rather than opportunistic hacking. The progression from Unitronics PLCs to Rockwell Automation controllers, from defacement to process manipulation, and from water systems to energy and telecommunications reflects systematic operational planning consistent with IRGC institutional direction. The capability to feed operators false readings while altering actual process logic is the threshold capability for a destructive cyber attack on physical infrastructure. Whether and when this capability is employed for destructive effect is assessed as a policy decision within the IRGC command structure, likely calibrated to the broader war trajectory. The dual-chokepoint escalation of September 10-11 does not directly trigger cyber escalation, but it indicates Iran's escalation calculus is increasingly aggressive.

5.2 October 7 Anniversary Threat Window

[E-MODERATE / P-MODERATE] The third anniversary of the October 7, 2023, Hamas attack on Israel falls approximately 25 days from the reporting date. Israeli Channel 12 reported intensified IRGC Quds Force and Hamas plotting against Israeli and Jewish targets abroad ahead of this date. Israeli security services assessed in the reporting period that Iranian attacks on Israeli or Jewish targets abroad are among the escalation options Tehran may be considering. This is characterized as a standing Israeli assessment rather than a new threat, but its restatement in the current environment carries analytical weight. The DHS NTAS bulletin of June 22, 2025, which specifically warned that the ongoing Israel-Iran conflict could contribute to US-based individuals plotting additional attacks and that multiple recent Homeland terrorist attacks have been motivated by anti-Semitic or anti-Israel sentiment, expired September 22, 2025. No publicly available renewal covering the current threat environment has been identified. This represents a gap in federal threat communication to state and local partners. [OSINT-PRESS: Channel 12 Israel; OSINT-GOV: DHS NTAS Jun 2025; OSINT-RESEARCH: CFR]

5.3 Federal Homeland Security Capacity Assessment

[E-MODERATE] / [P-LOW] The Council on Foreign Relations assessed in March 2026 that DHS capacity to fulfill its counterterrorism mission may be degraded by the administration's redirection of resources toward immigration enforcement, workforce cuts affecting counterterrorism expertise, and leadership turnover across key agencies. The CFR analysis noted that the expertise, knowledge, and institutional memory assembled on counterterrorism and homeland security over the past twenty-five years in DHS and in the FBI and U.S. Intelligence Community could have been lost in recent workforce cuts. The CSIS analysis of Iranian cyber threats (May 2026) similarly noted capacity concerns. This assessment cannot be independently verified through OSINT; it represents expert institutional analysis rather than sourced operational reporting. However, the absence of a renewed NTAS bulletin covering the active war environment is consistent with degraded federal threat communication capacity. This assessment assumes DHS resource allocation decisions are publicly visible to some degree; classified reallocations could change this conclusion. [OSINT-RESEARCH: CFR Mar 2026, CSIS May 2026]

Section 6: Protective Guidance

Critical Infrastructure Operators (Water, Energy, Government)

Review CISA Advisory AA26-097A (updated July 22, 2026) and implement all recommended mitigations for Rockwell Automation PLCs immediately. Specific actions: verify no default credentials remain on internet-facing PLCs; implement multifactor authentication for OT network access from external networks; create and test offline backups of PLC logic and configurations; monitor for unauthorized changes to ladder logic using CISA-provided indicators of compromise. Validate that HMI displays reflect actual process states by cross-referencing with independent physical sensors where available.

Telecommunications Providers

NBC September 2026 reporting indicates expanded IRGC-affiliated targeting of telecom networks. Monitor for reconnaissance activity consistent with CyberAv3ngers TTPs. Coordinate with FBI and CISA for sector-specific threat briefings.

Facilities Associated with Jewish, Israeli, or U.S. Government Interests

The October 7 anniversary (approximately 25 days) opens a threat window for both directed and inspired attacks. Observable pre-attack indicators include: unusual surveillance activity (photography of security measures, timing of guard rotations, vehicle access points); online radicalization indicators in community monitoring; direct threats referencing the anniversary or the Iran war. Coordinate with local FBI Joint Terrorism Task Forces and fusion centers. Review and test physical security protocols, particularly access control and active threat response plans.

Corporate Security Directors with Gulf Region Exposure

The dual-chokepoint crisis directly affects energy supply chains, logistics, and personnel safety for organizations with Gulf state operations. Review evacuation and shelter-in-place plans for regional personnel. Assess supply chain vulnerabilities tied to Strait of Hormuz and Red Sea disruption. Monitor travel advisories for Bahrain, Kuwait, Jordan, and Saudi Arabia, all of which have experienced Iranian kinetic operations during the conflict.

Section 7: Assessment and Outlook

24-72 Hours

[E-MODERATE / P-MODERATE] No specific, credible Homeland threat was identified in the reporting period. The immediate risk is a continuation of the baseline elevated threat environment driven by Iranian cyber operations and the approaching October 7 anniversary. The dual-chokepoint escalation does not create an immediate Homeland trigger but raises the overall retaliatory calculus. The most likely Homeland activity in this window is continued CyberAv3ngers reconnaissance and exploitation attempts against U.S. OT networks.

7-14 Days

[E-MODERATE / P-MODERATE] The October 7 anniversary window opens in approximately 25 days. Pre-operational activity (surveillance, recruitment, acquisition) for anniversary-timed attacks would be occurring now if planned. The absence of specific indicators in OSINT does not preclude classified reporting. If the dual-chokepoint crisis triggers a major U.S. military escalation (e.g., direct strikes on Houthi positions, strikes on Iranian nuclear facilities), the Homeland retaliatory calculus shifts materially upward.

Strategic (30+ Days)

[E-LOW / P-LOW] If Trump's midterm timeline statement reflects genuine policy intent, the war could enter a de-escalation phase within 60 days, which would gradually reduce the Homeland threat. If the statement is political messaging and the war continues through November and beyond, the accumulated duration of conflict increases the statistical probability of a Homeland event, whether directed, enabled, or inspired. The CyberAv3ngers capability to execute destructive attacks on U.S. infrastructure represents a persistent strategic risk regardless of the kinetic war trajectory.

Downgrade Criteria

The Homeland threat assessment would be revised downward if: a verified ceasefire includes an explicit Iranian commitment to cease cyber operations against U.S. infrastructure, confirmed by a period of observable cessation; the October 7 anniversary passes without incident or elevated threat reporting; the FBI or DHS publicly assess the Homeland threat as reduced.

Upgrade Criteria

The assessment would be revised upward to HIGH if: the FBI or DHS issues a specific, credible threat warning for a Homeland target; CISA reports a successful destructive cyber attack on U.S. critical infrastructure attributed to Iranian actors; a kinetic attack or disrupted plot targeting U.S. persons or facilities is attributed to IRGC or proxy networks; the U.S. strikes Iranian nuclear facilities, which would represent the highest-probability trigger for Homeland retaliation.
